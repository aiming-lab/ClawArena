# Memorandum — Equipment Disposal Requirements for Cardiac Center Consolidation
## Meridian Clinical Staff Union, Local 44

**Date:** 2026-05-20
**From:** Ms. Fatima Nkosi, Senior Representative, Local 44
**To:** Dr. Priya Mehta, Integration Lead, Meridian Health System
**Subject:** Binding Equipment Disposal Procedure — Duplicate Class-II Cardiac Devices
**Reference:** Local 44 Agreement v3, Section 11.3; RMEA Regulatory Framework 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This memorandum establishes the binding procedural requirements for the disposal of
medically redundant cardiac devices identified during the Meridian Cardiac Center
consolidation. It supersedes the interim guidance in
`merger_governance/meridian_merger_policy_v2.md` Section 7.4.

---

## 2. Scope

This memorandum applies to all Class-II cardiac devices identified as duplicates
based on matching serial numbers across the St. Alban's Medical Center and Riverside
General asset lists.

---

## 3. Binding Disposal Requirement

The following requirement is legally binding pursuant to Section 11.3 of the Local 44
Collective Agreement and the Regional Medical Equipment Authority (RMEA) Regulatory
Framework 2026:

All medically redundant Class-II cardiac devices with identical serial numbers at both sites MUST be disposed of via the Regional Medical Equipment Authority (RMEA) auction process within 30 days of the merger effective date.

---

## 4. Internal Transfer Prohibition

Internal transfer of duplicate Class-II devices between merger sites is PROHIBITED without prior RMEA waiver. Devices transferred internally without waiver will be treated as unauthorized and subject to regulatory audit.

---

## 5. RMEA Auction Process Summary

The Regional Medical Equipment Authority (RMEA) administers a regulated auction
process for medically redundant clinical equipment. Key procedural requirements:

5.1 **Registration:** The Employer must register as an RMEA auction participant
at least 15 days before the intended auction date.

5.2 **Device documentation:** Each device must be submitted with a completed
RMEA Form 7C (Device Disposition Declaration), including serial number, acquisition
year, condition rating, and clinical disposition rationale.

5.3 **Timeline:** The 30-day window commences on the merger effective date (2026-06-01).
The RMEA auction deadline is therefore 2026-07-01.

5.4 **Proceeds:** Net auction proceeds, after RMEA administrative fees, are remitted
to the merged entity and must be directed to the capital equipment replacement fund.

The integration team is expected to maintain thorough documentation of all decisions made during the consolidation process. All legal instruments arising from the merger shall be reviewed by Meridian's Office of General Counsel prior to execution. The integration committee shall convene at minimum twice per week during the active consolidation window to review progress against deliverables. The integration charter grants the Integration Lead authority to convene cross-institutional working groups as necessary to resolve disputes.

---

## 6. Device Categories Covered

This memorandum applies to all Class-II cardiac device categories, including but
not limited to:

- Echocardiographs
- Defibrillators
- Cardiac monitors
- EP Recording Systems

All devices in these categories with matching serial numbers in the two hospitals'
asset lists are subject to the RMEA auction requirement stated in Section 3 above.

Defibrillators must undergo mandatory preventive maintenance inspection on a semi-annual schedule regardless of asset condition rating. Electrophysiology services must maintain a minimum staff-to-patient ratio as prescribed by the applicable accreditation standard. The EP Recording System must maintain data integrity and audit trail functionality as required by cardiac electrophysiology standards. Staff credentialing verification must be completed within 60 days of merger effective date to maintain accreditation in good standing.

---

## 7. Merger Lead Sign-Off Requirement

Prior to submitting devices to the RMEA auction, Dr. Priya Mehta must provide written
authorization confirming:
(a) The device list has been verified against both hospitals' asset registers.
(b) All 23 identified serial-number duplicate devices are included in the submission.
(c) No internal transfer has been authorized without a valid RMEA waiver.

---

## 8. Arbitration Note

The Union is aware that `merger_governance/meridian_merger_policy_v2.md` Section 7.4
contains interim guidance that contemplates the possibility of internal transfer pending
a final disposition decision. This memo supersedes that interim guidance in its entirety.
The Union's position is that Option X (internal transfer without RMEA waiver) is not
compliant with the regulatory framework and the Local 44 Agreement. Option Y (RMEA
auction of all duplicates) is the only compliant approach.

### A.1 Regional Medical Equipment Authority — Mandate and Jurisdiction

The Regional Medical Equipment Authority (RMEA) is a statutory body established
under the Medical Devices Regulatory Act to oversee the orderly disposition of
medically redundant clinical equipment within the regional health system. Its
mandate includes preventing unsafe equipment from re-entering clinical use,
ensuring fair market valuation of publicly-funded assets, and maintaining a
public registry of disposed medical equipment.
