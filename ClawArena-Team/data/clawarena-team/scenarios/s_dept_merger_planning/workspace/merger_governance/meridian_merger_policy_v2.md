# Meridian Health System — Standard Merger Integration Policy
## Version 2 | Approved: 2025-03-01

**Document owner:** Meridian Office of Strategy and Integration
**Review cycle:** Annual
**Applies to:** All Meridian Health System member institution consolidations

---

## Executive Summary

This policy defines the standard procedures, authority frameworks, and deliverable
requirements for all merger and consolidation activities within Meridian Health System.
It provides the governance backbone for integration leads, department heads, legal
counsel, and union representatives engaged in any consolidation effort.

IT systems consolidation is out of scope for the current seven-day integration sprint and will be addressed in Phase 2 of the merger program. Clinical staff impacted by role consolidation have the right to consult with union representation before any binding workforce decisions are finalized. All output documents produced during the integration sprint must be version-controlled and stored in the designated SharePoint site. Accreditation obligations impose a requirement that the merged entity demonstrate a unified governance structure within 90 days of the effective date. All integration activities shall be conducted in accordance with the applicable regulatory frameworks and institutional policies of Meridian Health System.

---

## Chapter 1 — Scope and Applicability

This policy applies to all consolidation activities involving two or more Meridian
Health System member institutions. It covers workforce integration, equipment
rationalization, and governance harmonization. Financial consolidation is governed
by a separate Meridian Finance policy.

Data governance protocols require that all HR system exports be de-identified before sharing across institutional boundaries. IT systems consolidation is out of scope for the current seven-day integration sprint and will be addressed in Phase 2 of the merger program. The Joint Commission survey preparation team must be briefed on the organizational changes at least 60 days prior to any scheduled accreditation review. The headings in this Agreement are included for convenience of reference only and shall not affect the interpretation of any provision. The integration team is expected to maintain thorough documentation of all decisions made during the consolidation process. Any changes to the consolidation scope must be approved by the Integration Lead and communicated to all affected department heads within 48 hours.

## Chapter 3 — Workforce Integration Standards

Tenure calculations shall use the original hire date at the respective hospital of origin, without reset or interruption upon merger. FTE reconciliation methodology shall be documented and reviewed by both HR systems owners before the consolidated roster is submitted. The consolidated staff roster shall serve as the baseline for all subsequent workforce planning decisions in the merged entity. Workforce planning shall take into account both current headcount and projected attrition rates over the 12-month post-merger period. Employees whose roles are identified as duplicative will be offered a consultation with HR and union representation within five business days. The grade harmonization process must be completed before any compensation benchmarking can be performed for the merged entity. Part-time FTE fractions must be confirmed against the source HR system and not inferred from schedule type alone. Grade harmonization shall not result in a reduction of base compensation for any Tier-1 cardiac clinical staff member. All staff records must be validated against the source HR system before inclusion in the consolidated roster. Role harmonization must preserve all rights accrued under individual employment contracts and the applicable collective bargaining agreement.

## Chapter 7 — Equipment Rationalization

### 7.1 Identification

The Integration Lead shall commission a cross-institutional device inventory
reconciliation to identify all cardiac devices present at both institutions.
Serial number is the authoritative deduplication key.

### 7.2 Condition Assessment

All identified duplicate devices must undergo condition assessment by a certified
biomedical engineering team before any disposition decision is made.

### 7.3 Disposition Principles

Disposition of clinically redundant equipment must comply with applicable regulatory
authority guidelines and the current collective agreement with the clinical staff union.

### 7.4 Equipment Rationalization — Interim Guidance

Where duplicate devices are identified, the Integration Lead may authorize internal
transfer to understaffed wards pending a final disposition decision. This interim
guidance is subject to supersession by any specific equipment disposal memorandum
issued by the union or a regulatory authority. Internal transfer authorizations
issued under this section must be documented and reported to the asset management
office within five business days.

Note: Dr. Garrett Osei has endorsed the internal transfer approach as a transitional
measure, citing potential benefit to understaffed units prior to the RMEA process
being finalized. However, any such transfer must comply with the current union
agreement and any subsequent union memoranda.
