# Lessons Learned from Prior Meridian Health System Mergers

**Document owner:** Meridian Office of Strategy and Integration
**Date:** 2025-01-15
**Applies to:** All future Meridian consolidation projects as background reference

---

## Introduction

This document compiles lessons learned from three prior Meridian Health System
consolidation projects. None of these projects involved cardiac care services;
the current 2026 Cardiac Center consolidation is the first of its type within
Meridian. The lessons herein are provided as general guidance.

---

## Lesson 1 — Start With the Data Schema

Prior consolidations revealed that HR data schema differences between merging
institutions are consistently the most time-consuming challenge. Agreeing on a
common data dictionary early in the integration sprint reduces rework significantly.

Part-time FTE fractions must be confirmed against the source HR system and not inferred from schedule type alone. FTE reconciliation methodology shall be documented and reviewed by both HR systems owners before the consolidated roster is submitted. All staff records must be validated against the source HR system before inclusion in the consolidated roster. Grade harmonization shall not result in a reduction of base compensation for any Tier-1 cardiac clinical staff member. The grade harmonization process must be completed before any compensation benchmarking can be performed for the merged entity.

---

## Lesson 2 — Union Engagement Timing

Engaging the union representative early and providing access to working documents
reduces disputes late in the integration process. In the 2023 Meridian Orthopedics
consolidation, late union consultation resulted in a 14-day delay in finalizing the
workforce plan.

The integration committee shall convene at minimum twice per week during the active consolidation window to review progress against deliverables. The integration charter grants the Integration Lead authority to convene cross-institutional working groups as necessary to resolve disputes. The Joint Commission survey preparation team must be briefed on the organizational changes at least 60 days prior to any scheduled accreditation review. The integration project management office shall maintain a risk register updated at each weekly checkpoint meeting. Clinical staff impacted by role consolidation have the right to consult with union representation before any binding workforce decisions are finalized.

---

## Lesson 3 — Archive Discipline

Documentation from prior merger attempts must be clearly marked as superseded and
kept separate from current working documents. In the 2022 Meridian Radiology
consolidation, a team member accidentally used a 2019 equipment list that predated
significant capital investment, resulting in an equipment count that was off by
approximately 15%.

IT systems consolidation is out of scope for the current seven-day integration sprint and will be addressed in Phase 2 of the merger program. The integration committee shall convene at minimum twice per week during the active consolidation window to review progress against deliverables. The Joint Commission survey preparation team must be briefed on the organizational changes at least 60 days prior to any scheduled accreditation review. All integration activities shall be conducted in accordance with the applicable regulatory frameworks and institutional policies of Meridian Health System. Financial impact modeling for all staffing scenarios must be reviewed by the Meridian CFO before any reduction-in-force decisions are communicated.

---

## Lesson 4 — Org Chart as a Political Artifact

Org-chart harmonization decisions are rarely purely structural. Each Director title
holder and their teams have institutional identity tied to the org chart position.
Allow adequate time for stakeholder consultation on governance structure.

Any changes to the consolidation scope must be approved by the Integration Lead and communicated to all affected department heads within 48 hours. All legal instruments arising from the merger shall be reviewed by Meridian's Office of General Counsel prior to execution. Equipment condition ratings must be verified by an independent biomedical engineering assessment before any disposition determination is made. Stakeholder communication shall follow the escalation matrix established in Appendix C of the Meridian Integration Governance Framework. A post-integration review will be conducted ninety days after the merger effective date to assess operational continuity and accreditation status.

## Lesson 5 — Equipment Audit Prior to Disposition

Equipment inventories maintained by hospital asset management systems are frequently
out of date. Always verify the physical presence and condition of equipment before
relying on the asset list for disposition planning.

IT systems consolidation is out of scope for the current seven-day integration sprint and will be addressed in Phase 2 of the merger program. Equipment condition ratings must be verified by an independent biomedical engineering assessment before any disposition determination is made. Staff credentialing verification must be completed within 60 days of merger effective date to maintain accreditation in good standing. The integration team is expected to maintain thorough documentation of all decisions made during the consolidation process. All cardiac devices must display a current certification tag confirming the most recent inspection date and the next scheduled service.
