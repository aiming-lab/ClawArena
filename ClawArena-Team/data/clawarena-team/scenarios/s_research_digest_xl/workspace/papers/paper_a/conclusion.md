# 6. Conclusion

We presented SparseFormer, a transformer architecture that uses a learned adaptive locality prior to generate per-query sparsity masks, materialising attention only on the bucket-aligned subset of keys most likely to matter. The design delivers a 40% reduction in attention VRAM at 32k context (rising to 58% at 128k), a 1.43–1.92× decoding throughput improvement, and 97.3% retention of dense-attention accuracy across seven downstream benchmarks — all without destabilising pretraining. Our analytical bound on approximation error tightens as the empirical attention distribution becomes more peaked, which matches what we observe in practice on trained models. We hope SparseFormer encourages further work on architectures that adapt their computation to the empirical structure of the data rather than imposing a fixed sparsity pattern, and we release pretrained checkpoints and the custom kernel under a permissive licence to support reproducibility and downstream research.
