# Legal Opinion: Binding View-Count Metric in Chinese MCN Contracts

**Opinion Reference:** Media Law Chamber, Beijing Advanced Arbitration Institute,
Legal Opinion No. 2025-MLA-089
**Subject:** Contractually Binding View Count Metrics in Chinese MCN-Creator Agreements
**Date:** 2025-10-30
**Prepared for:** General MCN industry guidance; applicable to all active MCN-creator
  partnership agreements referencing platform-certified view count metrics.

---

Opinion Reference: Media Law Chamber, Beijing Advanced Arbitration Institute, Legal Opinion No. 2025-MLA-089, 'Contractually Binding View Count Metrics in Chinese MCN-Creator Agreements.' The following represents the chamber's consensus opinion as of 2025-10-30. This opinion has been cited in three subsequent arbitration decisions and is recognized as authoritative guidance by the China Internet Audiovisual Program Service Association.

Section 1: Platform-Certified Unique Deduplicated Views as the Standard Metric. Following a review of thirty-seven MCN-creator agreements executed between 2023 and 2025, the chamber finds that the industry standard for KPI-threshold measurement in short-video MCN contracts is the platform's certified unique deduplicated view count. This metric is defined consistently across major Chinese short-video platforms as a per-user, per-video, per-day count excluding inauthentic views. MCN partners who present view count figures based on raw data, blended methodologies, or non-platform-standard aggregation methods bear the burden of demonstrating contractual authorization for such methodologies.

Section 2: Purchased Traffic and Its Effect on KPI Computation. Views attributable to purchased traffic ('刷量') must be excluded from any KPI assessment regardless of the view count column used. The platform's fraud detection system provides the authoritative identification of purchased traffic via the purchased_traffic_flag field in the backend export. An MCN that presents view count figures inclusive of purchased traffic for KPI assessment purposes commits a material misrepresentation under Article 6 of the General Provisions of Civil Law of the People's Republic of China.

Section 3: Algorithm Updates as KPI Renegotiation Triggers. Where a platform algorithm update causes a systemic reduction in organic traffic exceeding 10% across the affected content category, affected creators have a cognizable right to request KPI threshold renegotiation. This right exists independently of any oral commitment made by the MCN account manager. An MCN's refusal to renegotiate KPI thresholds following a documented systemic algorithm update may constitute a breach of its implied obligation to deal in good faith under the Chinese Contract Law.

Section 4: Oral Commitments Made in Recorded Meetings. The chamber affirms the enforceability of oral commitments made by MCN account managers in recorded video meetings where: (a) the commitment is sufficiently specific regarding the threshold adjustment trigger and the adjusted threshold value; (b) the account manager held apparent authority to commit the MCN on the relevant matter; and (c) the creator provided or withheld consideration in reliance on the commitment. A verbatim audio recording of the commitment, when authenticated, constitutes primary evidence admissible in arbitration proceedings under CIETAC arbitration rules.
