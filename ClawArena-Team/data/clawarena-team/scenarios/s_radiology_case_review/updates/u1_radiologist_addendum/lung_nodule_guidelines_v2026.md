## Preamble

These guidelines are issued to all clinical staff involved in the detection, management, and follow-up of pulmonary nodules identified on CT imaging. They incorporate updates from the Fleischner Society 2017 white paper, the British Thoracic Society guidelines 2023, and institutional data from the local lung nodule registry (n=2,847 patients, 2019–2025).

---

## Background and Epidemiology

Pulmonary nodules are defined as small, roughly spherical lesions measuring ≤ 30 mm in diameter seen on CT imaging. They are detected in 1–51% of CT studies depending on population risk factors. The majority (> 95%) are benign, but a small proportion represent early-stage lung malignancy, for which early detection and intervention is associated with markedly improved survival.

## Classification by Size

Nodules are classified by greatest axial diameter as measured on lung-window CT: Subcentimetre (< 6 mm): very low risk, no routine follow-up unless high-risk features. Small (6–8 mm): low-intermediate risk, consider 6-month follow-up CT. Intermediate (8–15 mm): biopsy threshold approached; requires careful characterisation. Large (> 15 mm): high suspicion; tissue sampling strongly indicated unless benign features.

## Nodule Characterisation — Morphological Features

Ground-glass opacity (GGO): hyper-attenuating areas without obscuring underlying vessels/bronchi; may represent AAH, AIS, or MIA. Part-solid: contains both GGO and solid components; higher malignancy risk. Solid: entirely homogeneous soft-tissue density; broad differential. Calcification patterns: central (granuloma), diffuse (hamartoma), popcorn (hamartoma), laminar (benign); eccentric or amorphous calcification does NOT exclude malignancy. Spiculation / corona radiata: highly suspicious for malignancy (~88% PPV in meta-analyses). Lobulation: irregular margin without spiculation; intermediate suspicion.

## Fleischner Society Guidelines 2017 — Summary

Solid nodule in low-risk individual: < 6 mm no routine follow-up; 6–8 mm CT at 6–12 months; > 8 mm consider CT at 3 months, PET, or tissue sampling. Solid nodule in high-risk individual: < 6 mm CT at 12 months; 6–8 mm CT at 6–12 months; > 8 mm as above. Subsolid nodule (GGO): < 6 mm no follow-up; ≥ 6 mm CT at 6–12 months. Part-solid nodule: ≥ 6 mm with solid component CT at 3–6 months; if solid component ≥ 6 mm, consider tissue sampling.

## Institutional Biopsy Criteria

CT-guided percutaneous biopsy is indicated when: (1) nodule size > 8 mm AND irregular/spiculated margin; OR (2) metabolic activity on PET (SUVmax > 2.5); OR (3) rapid growth (VDT < 400 days); OR (4) Lung-RADS category 4X. Pre-procedure requirements: INR < 1.5, platelet count > 50,000/μL, eGFR > 30 mL/min/1.73m², spirometry if borderline lung function. Antiplatelet agents: aspirin held 5 days, clopidogrel held 5–7 days. NOAC: held per standard peri-procedural protocol.

## PET-CT Indications

FDG PET-CT is recommended when: CT characterisation is indeterminate (Lung-RADS 3–4A) AND nodule size ≥ 8 mm; OR lesion has grown on surveillance CT but CT morphology remains non-specific; OR prior to biopsy to identify most metabolically active sub-region. Pitfalls: false-negative in GGO/part-solid lesions (lower FDG avidity), mucinous adenocarcinoma, carcinoid. False-positive: active granuloma, rheumatoid nodule, organising pneumonia.

## AI-Assisted Detection — Validation and Limitations

AI detection tools for pulmonary nodules have shown sensitivity of 85–94% and specificity of 80–91% in validation studies. Key limitations: (1) Lobe attribution errors: current commercial tools misattribute lobe in 3–8% of cases, particularly at fissural locations and in incomplete fissures. (2) Size discrepancy: AI measurements may differ from radiologist measurements by ±2 mm; always use radiologist-confirmed measurements for clinical decision-making. (3) AI summaries lack clinical context: automated outputs do not incorporate patient history, prior imaging, or clinical symptoms. Institutional policy: AI outputs are adjuncts ONLY. Final lobe attribution and clinical decision must be confirmed by a qualified radiologist. Known issue in this department: radiology-bot v1.7 has a documented lobe mis-attribution bug affecting right lower / left upper lobe boundary cases.

## Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) Process

All nodules meeting biopsy criteria are discussed at weekly Lung MDT (Friday 07:30). Pre-MDT preparation: referring physician submits case summary ≥ 48h before meeting. MDT composition: Respiratory medicine, Thoracic surgery, Radiology, Oncology, Pathology (on request), Nuclear medicine (on request). Post-MDT: decision letter sent to GP within 5 working days. Patient communication: informed of MDT decision within 10 working days. Documentation: all MDT decisions entered into the Electronic Health Record.

## Radiation Dose Considerations

Lung cancer screening CT protocols aim for CTDIvol < 3 mGy. Diagnostic thoracic CT for nodule characterisation: CTDIvol 4–8 mGy typical. CT-guided biopsy: intermittent fluoroscopy, total estimated dose 5–15 mGy per procedure. PET-CT: ~7 mSv effective dose. Cumulative radiation dose tracking: all patients referred for lung surveillance should have cumulative dose documented per national DRL audit requirements.

## Documentation and Structured Reporting

All CT reports for pulmonary nodules must include: (1) Lobe and segment localisation (right/left; upper/middle/lower; segment number). (2) Size in greatest axial dimension (lung window measurement). (3) Morphological descriptors: solid/GGO/part-solid, margin character, calcification. (4) Comparison with prior study if available: absolute and relative size change. (5) Lung-RADS or BRAF-2 category assignment. (6) Recommended action: no follow-up / surveillance interval / PET / biopsy. Structured template: available in RIS as 'Lung Nodule Template v3.2 (2026)'. AI output disclaimer: if AI tool was used, state 'AI-assisted detection — results confirmed and may be modified by reporting radiologist'.
