INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT — BACKGROUND
Subject: Dr. Amara Osei, Procurement Law Specialist, Riverbend University
Interviewer: Marcus Delacroix
Date: 2024-10-09
Off-record background only — not for direct quotation

Delacroix: What does Section 47 require in terms of board member disclosure?

Osei: Section 47 of the Public Procurement Act 2021 is unambiguous. Any
individual who simultaneously holds a material financial stake in a bidder
and sits on the board of the awarding or co-awarding body must formally
declare that conflict before the evaluation process begins. They must then
physically absent themselves from every meeting where that tender is discussed.
A mere verbal statement of intent is insufficient — the abstention must be
recorded in the official vote minutes.

Delacroix: What happens if that was not done?

Osei: The award is voidable. The contracting authority can face a compliance
order from the Public Accounts Committee. The individual concerned may face
a civil penalty under Regulation 18 of the Anti-Corruption Framework
Ordinance. In serious cases, criminal proceedings are possible.

Delacroix: And if the person claims they abstained verbally but the minutes
do not record it?

Osei: The minutes are the legal record. A verbal claim without a documented
abstention is not a valid defence. Period.

[end of background interview]
