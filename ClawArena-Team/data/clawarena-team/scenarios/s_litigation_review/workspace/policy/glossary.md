# HR Policy Glossary

**Revision:** 2.1 | **Effective:** January 1, 2025 | **Owner:** People Operations

---

## A

**At-Will Employment**
An employment arrangement in which either the employer or the employee may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any reason not prohibited by law, with or without notice. Applicable in jurisdictions that have not legislated otherwise.

**Accrual Rate**
The rate at which an employee accumulates leave benefits (vacation, sick leave, etc.) over time, typically expressed as hours per pay period or days per year.

**Adverse Employment Action**
Any employer action that materially and negatively affects the terms, conditions, or privileges of employment, including termination, demotion, reduction in pay, involuntary transfer, or significant reduction in work hours.

---

## B

**Base Pay**
The fixed, regular compensation paid to an employee, exclusive of overtime, bonuses, commissions, and other supplemental earnings.

**Blackout Period**
A designated timeframe during which employee requests for leave or schedule changes may be restricted due to operational demands (e.g., year-end financial close, peak business season).

---

## C

**Chain of Command**
The hierarchical structure through which authority and responsibility flow within an organization. Grievances and escalations should follow the established chain of command unless the matter involves the direct supervisor.

**Corrective Action**
A formal process used to address employee conduct or performance deficiencies, ranging from verbal warnings to written reprimands, performance improvement plans, and termination.

**COBRA**
Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act. Federal law that allows employees and their dependents to continue group health coverage for a limited period after qualifying events such as termination or reduction in hours.

---

## D

**Demotion**
A reduction in an employee's job title, responsibilities, pay grade, or a combination thereof. May be voluntary or involuntary.

**Documentation**
Written records related to employment actions, including performance reviews, corrective action notices, leave requests, and accommodations. HR retains documentation per the Records Retention Schedule.

---

## E

**EAP (Employee Assistance Program)**
A confidential, employer-funded program providing short-term counseling, mental health referrals, financial planning, and legal consultation services to employees and their immediate family members.

**EEOC**
Equal Employment Opportunity Commission. The federal agency responsible for enforcing federal laws prohibiting employment discrimination.

**Exempt Employee**
An employee who meets the criteria under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) to be excluded from overtime pay requirements, typically based on salary level and job duties.

---

## F

**FLSA**
Fair Labor Standards Act. Federal law establishing minimum wage, overtime pay, recordkeeping, and child labor standards.

**FMLA**
Family and Medical Leave Act. Federal law providing eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year for specified family and medical reasons.

---

## G

**Grievance**
A formal complaint filed by an employee alleging a violation of company policy, the employment agreement, or applicable law. Grievances are subject to the procedures outlined in the Employee Handbook.

**Gross Misconduct**
Severe violations of workplace standards that may result in immediate termination without prior corrective action, including but not limited to theft, workplace violence, harassment, and falsification of records.

---

## I

**Insubordination**
The deliberate refusal of an employee to follow a reasonable, lawful directive from a supervisor or manager. A basis for corrective action up to and including termination.

---

## N

**Non-Exempt Employee**
An employee who is entitled to overtime pay at a rate of at least one and one-half times the regular rate of pay for hours worked in excess of 40 per workweek, pursuant to the FLSA.

---

## O

**Open Enrollment**
The annual period during which employees may enroll in or make changes to their benefits elections. Changes outside of open enrollment are generally only permitted following a qualifying life event.

---

## P

**Pay Period**
The recurring time interval for which employee wages are calculated and disbursed. The company operates on a bi-weekly pay schedule.

**Progressive Discipline**
A structured corrective action process in which the severity of disciplinary measures escalates in response to repeated or serious violations, typically progressing from verbal warning through written warning, final warning, and termination.

**Probationary Period**
A defined initial period of employment (typically 90 days) during which the employee's suitability for the role is evaluated. Different policy provisions may apply during this period.

---

## R

**Records Retention Schedule**
The company's policy governing how long various categories of employment records must be maintained before permissible destruction. HR-related records are generally retained for seven years post-separation.

---

## S

**Separation Agreement**
A legally binding contract between an employer and a departing employee that typically includes terms of separation, severance arrangements, and mutual releases of claims.

**Severance Pay**
Compensation provided to an employee upon involuntary termination, calculated per the Severance Pay Policy based on tenure and classification.

---

## T

**Termination for Cause**
Termination resulting from the employee's conduct or performance, rather than business conditions. Documentation of the basis for termination is required prior to initiating the separation.

---

## W

**Workweek**
The company's standard workweek runs Sunday through Saturday for payroll calculation purposes.
