# Workplace Safety Training Module

**Module ID:** TRN-SAF-03 | **Version:** 2.3 | **Effective:** January 1, 2025
**Audience:** All employees | **Completion Required:** Annually and upon hire | **Duration:** Approximately 45 minutes
**Regulatory Basis:** OSHA General Industry Standards (29 CFR Part 1910)

---

## Introduction

A safe workplace is a shared responsibility. This module provides an overview of our workplace safety program, your rights and responsibilities under applicable regulations, emergency procedures, and the process for reporting hazards and incidents. Every employee — regardless of role or tenure — is expected to complete this training and apply its principles daily.

---

## Section 1: Safety Program Overview

### 1.1 Commitment to Safety

Our organization is committed to providing a workplace free from recognized hazards that are likely to cause serious physical harm. This commitment is not aspirational — it is operationalized through written safety policies, regular inspections, employee training, and a responsive incident reporting system.

The Safety Committee, composed of representatives from HR, Facilities, and department management, meets quarterly to review safety performance data, investigate significant incidents, and approve updates to safety procedures.

### 1.2 OSHA Rights

Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970, employees have the right to:

- Work in conditions that meet OSHA standards.
- Receive training in a language and vocabulary they can understand.
- Review records of work-related injuries and illnesses (OSHA 300 Log).
- Request an OSHA inspection if they believe a hazardous condition exists.
- File a complaint with OSHA without fear of retaliation.

No employee will be disciplined or retaliated against for exercising their rights under OSHA.

## Section 2: Common Workplace Hazards

### 2.1 Slips, Trips, and Falls

Slips, trips, and falls are among the most common causes of workplace injury. Prevention measures include:

- Report spills immediately and place wet floor signs until the area is dry.
- Keep walkways and emergency exits clear of clutter, boxes, and equipment.
- Use handrails when using staircases.
- Wear appropriate footwear for your work environment.
- Report damaged flooring, loose carpet, or broken stair treads to Facilities immediately.

### 2.2 Ergonomics

Employees who work at computer workstations for extended periods should ensure their workstation is set up to minimize strain:

- Monitor top should be at or slightly below eye level.
- Keyboard and mouse should allow elbows to remain at approximately 90 degrees.
- Chair should provide lumbar support and allow feet to rest flat on the floor or on a footrest.
- Take micro-breaks (1–2 minutes every 30–45 minutes) to reduce repetitive strain.

Employees who experience musculoskeletal discomfort related to their workstation should contact HR to request an ergonomic assessment.

### 2.3 Fire Safety

All employees must be familiar with the fire evacuation plan for their assigned work location. Emergency exit routes are posted in all common areas. Key requirements:

- Never prop open fire doors.
- Know the location of the nearest fire extinguisher in your work area (most offices use ABC-rated dry chemical extinguishers).
- Do not use elevators during a fire emergency.
- Assembly points are designated in the parking structure at the north and south ends of the building. Account for your presence to the Floor Safety Warden.

Fire drills are conducted at least twice annually. All employees are required to participate.

### 2.4 Electrical Safety

- Do not overload electrical outlets or use extension cords as permanent wiring solutions.
- Report damaged cords, flickering lights, or unusual odors from electrical equipment to Facilities immediately.
- Do not attempt to repair electrical equipment unless you are a trained and authorized technician.

## Section 3: Emergency Procedures

### 3.1 Medical Emergencies

If an employee experiences a medical emergency:

1. Call 911 immediately.
2. Notify your manager and the front desk/reception.
3. If trained, provide first aid or CPR until emergency services arrive — do not move an injured person unless they are in immediate danger.
4. AED (Automated External Defibrillator) units are located at main reception and on each floor adjacent to the elevator bank.

### 3.2 Active Threat / Evacuation

In the event of an active threat or mandatory evacuation:

- Follow instructions from emergency personnel and building security.
- Do not gather personal belongings; proceed directly to the nearest designated emergency exit.
- If evacuation is not possible, move to an interior room, lock the door, silence your phone, and wait for instructions from law enforcement.
- After the immediate threat has passed, report to the designated assembly point for personnel accountability.

---

## Section 4: Incident Reporting

### 4.1 What to Report

All of the following must be reported, regardless of whether an injury occurred:

- Any work-related injury or illness, no matter how minor.
- Near-miss incidents (situations that could have caused injury but did not).
- Unsafe conditions or practices observed in the workplace.
- Damage to equipment that could create a hazard.

### 4.2 How to Report

Incidents are reported through the Safety Incident form (FAC-SAFE-01), available on the HR portal and at Facilities. Reports should be submitted within 24 hours of the incident or observation. For injuries requiring medical attention, notify your manager immediately and contact HR on the same day.

OSHA-recordable incidents (those resulting in days away from work, restricted duty, or medical treatment beyond first aid) are documented on the OSHA 300 Log maintained by Facilities. The annual OSHA 300A summary is posted in each work location during the required February 1 – April 30 posting period.

## Section 5: Module Completion

Upon completing this module, log your completion in the HR training portal. Annual recertification is required; you will receive a reminder 30 days before your renewal date. Employees working in specialized environments (laboratories, warehouses, construction sites) must also complete the role-specific safety supplement relevant to their work area.

Direct questions to the Facilities Safety Coordinator at safety@company.internal or through the HR portal.
