# Harassment Prevention Training Module

**Module ID:** TRN-HAR-02 | **Version:** 4.1 | **Effective:** February 1, 2025
**Audience:** All employees | **Completion Required:** Annually | **Duration:** Approximately 60 minutes
**Regulatory Basis:** Title VII of the Civil Rights Act; State Fair Employment and Housing Act

## Introduction

Workplace harassment is not merely a policy violation — it causes real harm to individuals and undermines the culture of respect and safety that every employee deserves. This module explains what harassment is, how to recognize it, what to do if you experience or witness it, and how the organization investigates and responds to complaints.

Completion of this module is mandatory for all employees, including management and senior leadership. Your digital acknowledgment at the end of this module will be recorded in your HR file.

## Section 1: Defining Harassment

### 1.1 Legal Framework

Harassment is a form of employment discrimination prohibited under federal law (Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, as amended) and applicable state statutes. The law recognizes harassment based on protected characteristics including:

- Race, color, and national origin
- Sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation, and gender identity)
- Religion
- Disability
- Age (40 and older)
- Genetic information

### 1.2 Types of Harassment

**Quid Pro Quo Harassment**
Occurs when a person in a position of authority conditions employment benefits — such as a favorable assignment, promotion, raise, or continued employment — on submission to unwelcome conduct. Even a single instance of quid pro quo conduct may constitute actionable harassment.

**Hostile Work Environment**
Occurs when unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic is severe or pervasive enough to create an environment that a reasonable person would consider intimidating, hostile, or abusive. Determining whether a hostile environment exists involves considering:

- The frequency of the conduct.
- The severity of the conduct.
- Whether the conduct is physically threatening or humiliating, or merely an offensive utterance.
- Whether the conduct unreasonably interferes with the employee's work performance.

No single dimension is dispositive; the totality of circumstances is evaluated.

### 1.3 Examples of Harassing Conduct

The following examples are illustrative, not exhaustive:

- Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature.
- Derogatory comments, jokes, or slurs about a person's race, ethnicity, religion, disability, age, or other protected characteristic.
- Displaying or distributing offensive images, posters, or materials.
- Persistent, unwelcome teasing, mimicking, or mockery related to a protected characteristic.
- Excluding a colleague from meetings, communications, or social activities on the basis of a protected characteristic.
- Retaliatory conduct following a complaint or participation in an investigation.

## Section 2: What Is Not Harassment

Not all workplace conflict or interpersonal tension rises to the level of unlawful harassment. The following, while potentially uncomfortable, are generally not harassment under the legal framework:

- A single rude comment or isolated incident that is not severe.
- Constructive criticism of work quality or performance delivered professionally.
- Disagreements about work assignments or business decisions.
- General workplace stress or conflict that is not tied to a protected characteristic.

This does not mean such conduct is acceptable or without consequence under our Code of Conduct — but it is important to distinguish conduct that triggers the harassment complaint process from conduct addressed through other channels.

## Section 3: Your Responsibilities

### 3.1 All Employees

Every employee is responsible for:

- Treating colleagues with respect and professionalism at all times.
- Refraining from conduct that could constitute harassment or contribute to a hostile environment.
- Reporting harassment they experience or witness promptly through available channels.
- Cooperating fully with any investigation of a harassment complaint.

### 3.2 Managers and Supervisors

Managers and supervisors bear heightened responsibility because harassment by a supervisor creates employer liability under a more stringent standard. Managers must:

- Model professional conduct at all times.
- Address potential harassment issues promptly, even absent a formal complaint.
- Refrain from discouraging employees from making complaints.
- Report any complaint or observation of potential harassment to HR immediately.
- Maintain confidentiality during investigations to the extent practicable.

Failure by a manager to fulfill these responsibilities may itself result in disciplinary action.

## Section 4: Reporting a Complaint

### 4.1 Reporting Channels

Employees who experience or witness harassment are encouraged to report promptly through any of the following channels:

1. **Direct Manager** — appropriate if the manager is not the subject of the complaint.
2. **HR Department** — available by email (hr@company.internal), telephone, or in-person appointment.
3. **Anonymous Hotline** — available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week; no identifying information is required. Reports submitted through the hotline are reviewed by HR within one business day.
4. **Skip-Level Manager** — if the complaint involves the direct manager.

There is no required order of escalation. Employees may go directly to HR or the anonymous hotline without first speaking to their manager.

### 4.2 What Happens After You Report

Upon receipt of a complaint, HR will:

1. Acknowledge receipt of the complaint within one business day.
2. Assess the complaint to determine the appropriate investigation approach.
3. Assign an investigator (internal HR or external, depending on the nature and scope of the complaint).
4. Notify the respondent that a complaint has been filed and that an investigation will be conducted.
5. Conduct a thorough, impartial investigation, including interviews of all relevant parties and review of documentary evidence.
6. Reach findings and, where warranted, recommend corrective action.
7. Notify the complainant and respondent of the outcome to the extent permitted by confidentiality obligations.

Investigations are generally completed within 30 business days of the complaint, though complex matters may require additional time.

---

## Section 5: Anti-Retaliation Policy

Retaliation against any employee who makes a good-faith harassment complaint, participates in an investigation, or opposes conduct they reasonably believe to be unlawful is strictly prohibited. Retaliation may include adverse employment actions, increased scrutiny, exclusion, or any other conduct intended to punish or deter protected activity.

Retaliation is itself a violation of company policy and applicable law, and will be investigated and addressed as a separate matter, independent of the outcome of the underlying harassment complaint.
