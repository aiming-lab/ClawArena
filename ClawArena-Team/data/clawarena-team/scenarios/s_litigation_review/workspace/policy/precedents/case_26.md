# Case Digest: Lindqvist v. Nordic Capital
# Docket No. 2019-EMP-10925


## Procedural History

This matter came before the Employment Tribunal on the claimant's application
for reinstatement and compensation following the termination of employment by
the respondent. The claimant alleged that the respondent had failed to comply
with its own Performance Improvement Plan policy, which required a minimum
duration of 45 calendar days, whereas the actual PIP lasted
only 14 days before the claimant was dismissed.

The Tribunal conducted a three-day evidentiary hearing at which both parties
presented witness testimony and documentary evidence. The claimant relied
principally on the respondent's employee handbook, the signed PIP form, and
the audio recording of the PIP kick-off meeting. The respondent disputed the
claimant's characterization of the applicable minimum duration and contended
that the claimant had been afforded a meaningful remediation opportunity.



## Factual Background

The claimant was employed by the respondent as a Operations Specialist
for a period of 9 years prior to the events giving rise to this complaint.
The respondent initiated a PIP on February 14, 2019,
citing performance deficiencies in stakeholder communication.

The PIP document was signed by the claimant, the direct supervisor, and an HR representative.
The claimant contends that representations made during the kick-off meeting as to the timeline
and evaluation criteria were not accurately reflected in the subsequent automatic transcript
generated by the respondent's meeting recording system.

## Tribunal's Holding

An employer who terminates an employee before the expiry of the minimum PIP duration specified in the employee handbook assumes the risk of wrongful termination liability.

## Disposition

The claimant's claim of constructive dismissal was upheld; damages were calculated on a twelve-month basis.
