# Part III — Wage and Hour Compliance

## 3.1 Minimum Wage

Employers must pay each non-exempt employee not less than the applicable
statutory minimum wage for each hour worked. Where federal, state, and local
minimum wage standards differ, the highest applicable rate governs. Tip credits,
service charges, and similar arrangements are subject to specific statutory
conditions and must be documented in writing.

## 3.2 Overtime

Non-exempt employees are entitled to overtime compensation at the rate of one
and one-half times the regular rate for hours worked in excess of forty (40)
in a workweek. Certain states impose daily overtime thresholds in addition to
the weekly threshold. The regular rate must include all forms of
non-discretionary compensation, including non-discretionary bonuses, shift
differentials, and certain incentive payments.

## 3.3 Hours of Work

Time during which an employee is required to remain on the employer's premises
or otherwise restricted in the employee's use of the time is generally
compensable. Preliminary and postliminary activities, on-call time, travel
time, and training time are subject to fact-specific analysis under the
applicable statute.

## 3.4 Recordkeeping

Employers must maintain accurate contemporaneous records of all hours worked,
wages paid, and deductions taken, for the periods prescribed by the applicable
statute. The failure to maintain such records gives rise to an evidentiary
presumption in favor of the employee's good-faith estimate of hours worked.

## 3.5 Pay Frequency and Final Pay

Wages must be paid on regular paydays not less frequently than the interval
prescribed by applicable law. On termination of employment, final wages —
including any accrued but unpaid amounts payable on separation — must be
paid within the statutory period, which varies by jurisdiction and by the
character of the separation (voluntary or involuntary).
