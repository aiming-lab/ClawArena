# Part VI — Termination, Severance, and Reinstatement

## 6.1 Lawful Grounds for Termination

Termination is lawful where it is supported by a substantively fair reason
(see Part V, Section 5.1), the procedural fairness requirements of Section
5.2 have been satisfied, and the termination does not contravene any
statutory protection — including anti-discrimination law, whistleblower
protection law, and any applicable collective bargaining agreement.

## 6.2 Notice and Severance

Statutory notice requirements vary by jurisdiction and by length of service.
Where the contract of employment specifies a notice period in excess of the
statutory minimum, the contractual notice period applies. Payment in lieu of
notice is generally permitted, subject to applicable wage payment law.

Severance pay, where required by statute or by contract, must be calculated
in accordance with the applicable formula and paid within the statutory or
contractual period. Voluntary severance arrangements are typically
documented in a severance agreement that includes a release of claims;
such releases are enforceable only where they satisfy the statutory
requirements of voluntariness, consideration, and informed waiver.

## 6.3 Reinstatement

Where a termination is found to have been wrongful — whether by reason of
substantive unfairness, procedural unfairness, or violation of a protected
characteristic — the available remedies typically include reinstatement to
the former position, back pay, and, in appropriate cases, front pay and
other compensatory damages.

A reinstatement order is enforceable through contempt proceedings. Where
reinstatement is not feasible (for example, where the position has been
eliminated or where the working relationship has irretrievably broken down),
the tribunal may order front pay in lieu of reinstatement.

## 6.4 Constructive Dismissal

A unilateral and material adverse change to the terms and conditions of
employment, imposed by the employer without the employee's consent, may
constitute a constructive dismissal — that is, a termination in fact even
though the employer has not formally communicated a separation. The
substantive and procedural fairness analysis applicable to express
terminations applies equally to constructive dismissal.

## 6.5 Post-Employment Obligations

Restrictive covenants — including non-competition, non-solicitation, and
confidentiality covenants — are enforceable to the extent they are
reasonable in scope, duration, and geographic reach, and supported by valid
consideration. The legal test varies materially by jurisdiction.
