# Part V — Performance Management and Termination

## 5.1 Substantive Fairness in Termination Decisions

A termination decision will withstand legal scrutiny only if it is substantively
fair — meaning that the stated reasons for termination are genuine, supported by
documented evidence, and proportionate to the employee's conduct or performance
deficiencies. Courts and administrative tribunals will examine whether the
employer's characterization of the employee's performance accurately reflects the
documented record.

A pretextual reason — that is, a stated reason that is shown by the evidence to
be not the true reason — is treated as no reason at all, and may give rise to an
inference of unlawful motive where the employee has invoked a protected
characteristic or protected activity.

## 5.2 Procedural Fairness Requirements

In addition to substantive fairness, termination decisions must satisfy procedural
fairness requirements. At a minimum, procedural fairness requires that:

(a) The employee was given adequate notice of the performance deficiencies or
    misconduct giving rise to the termination;
(b) The employee was afforded a reasonable opportunity to respond to or remediate
    the identified issues;
(c) The employer followed its own established procedures consistently;
(d) The decision was made by an appropriately authorized individual after
    consideration of all relevant information.

Where the employer's own handbook prescribes a procedural step — such as a PIP
mid-point review — and that step is omitted or perfunctorily performed, the
procedural fairness requirement is not satisfied.

## 5.3 Performance Improvement Plans — Legal Minimum Duration

Under the General Labor Standards adopted by the jurisdiction in which Acme
Corporation primarily operates, a formal performance improvement plan for a
full-time employee must have a minimum duration sufficient to constitute a bona
fide remediation opportunity. Pursuant to current regulatory guidance, this
minimum is no fewer than thirty (30) calendar days for full-time employees.

Where an employer's own written policy specifies a minimum PIP duration consistent
with or exceeding this regulatory floor, deviation from that policy — even if not
directly regulated — may create an implied contractual obligation enforceable
against the employer.

Note: This figure is subject to amendment by subsequent regulatory action.
HR and Legal Affairs personnel should monitor the regulatory environment for any
updates that may affect the applicable minimum duration. See also any addenda
issued to this compendium for the most current applicable figures.

## 5.4 Documentation Standards

All termination decisions, particularly those predicated on employee performance
or conduct, must be supported by contemporaneous written documentation. Retroactive
documentation — that is, documentation prepared after the fact to justify a
decision already made — is not only inadequate as an evidentiary matter but may
expose the employer to adverse inferences in subsequent proceedings.

HR personnel are reminded that electronic records, including emails, HRIS entries,
and digital signatures on PIP forms, constitute documentary evidence subject to
discovery in litigation and regulatory investigations.

## 5.5 Calculation of the PIP Period

For the purposes of Section 5.3 the PIP period runs from the date on which the
employee signs the PIP document acknowledging its terms (the "PIP commencement
date") to the date on which the employer first takes, or first communicates an
intention to take, adverse employment action predicated on the employee's
failure to meet the PIP objectives. A termination decision communicated to the
employee before the expiry of the applicable minimum period is presumptively
unlawful regardless of the calendar date on which the termination is given
effect.

Adverse employment action for the purposes of this Section includes
termination, involuntary demotion, involuntary transfer, and any other
material reduction in the terms and conditions of employment.

## 5.6 Burden of Proof

In a wrongful termination or breach-of-implied-contract proceeding predicated
on PIP non-compliance, the employer bears the burden of demonstrating that the
applicable minimum PIP duration was observed and that the employee was afforded
a bona fide remediation opportunity. Evidentiary gaps — such as missing
mid-point review documentation or inconsistent records of PIP commencement —
are construed against the employer.
