# Part I — Employment Relationship Fundamentals

## 1.1 At-Will Employment

In jurisdictions that recognize the at-will employment doctrine, either party to
an employment relationship may terminate that relationship at any time, for any
reason not prohibited by law, or for no reason at all, without prior notice
(subject to any contractual notice provisions). Notwithstanding the at-will
doctrine, employers remain bound by anti-discrimination statutes, public policy
exceptions, and any express or implied contractual commitments made to employees.

The at-will rule does not displace the duty of employers to comply with
statutory minimum standards governing termination, wages, hours, leave, and
non-discrimination. Where a statutory minimum standard and an at-will
employment relationship intersect, the statutory standard prevails.

## 1.2 Employee Handbooks as Implied Contracts

In many jurisdictions, the provisions of an employee handbook may give rise to
implied contractual obligations binding upon the employer. Where a handbook
specifies procedures that must be followed before adverse employment action may
be taken — such as minimum performance improvement plan durations — courts have
frequently held that deviation from those procedures may give rise to claims for
breach of implied contract, wrongful termination, or promissory estoppel.

Employers are therefore advised to include clear disclaimer language in handbooks
to the effect that the handbook does not create a contract of employment; however,
such disclaimers are not always effective, particularly where the employer has
engaged in a course of conduct inconsistent with the at-will doctrine.

## 1.3 Progressive Discipline and the Duty of Fair Dealing

Many jurisdictions have recognized an implied covenant of good faith and fair
dealing in employment relationships. Under this doctrine, employers may be
required to provide employees with reasonable notice of performance deficiencies,
a meaningful opportunity to remediate those deficiencies, and consistent
application of disciplinary standards across similarly situated employees.

The failure to afford an employee a genuine opportunity to improve performance
before termination — for example, by cutting short a performance improvement plan
before its stipulated minimum duration — may constitute a breach of this implied
covenant, rendering the termination wrongful.

## 1.4 Statutory Floor

The terms of an individual contract of employment and the provisions of an
employer's handbook are subject to a statutory floor. Where the statutory floor
is more protective of the employee than the contract or handbook, the statutory
rule applies. Where the contract or handbook is more protective than the
statutory floor, the contract or handbook governs.
