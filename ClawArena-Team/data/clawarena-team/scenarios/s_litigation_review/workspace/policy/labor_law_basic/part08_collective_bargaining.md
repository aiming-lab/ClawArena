# Part VIII — Collective Bargaining and Recordkeeping

## 8.1 Right to Organize

Employees have the right to form, join, and assist labor organizations and to
engage in concerted activity for the purpose of mutual aid and protection.
Employer interference with the exercise of these rights — including
surveillance, interrogation, promise of benefit, or threat of reprisal — is
an unfair labor practice.

## 8.2 Recognition and Bargaining

Where a labor organization is recognized or certified as the exclusive
bargaining representative for a unit of employees, the employer must bargain
in good faith with that representative concerning wages, hours, and other
terms and conditions of employment. The duty to bargain in good faith does
not require either party to make any particular concession or to reach
agreement.

## 8.3 Application to Performance Management

In a unionized environment, the employer's performance management
procedures — including PIP issuance and outcome determinations — may be
subject to grievance and arbitration under the applicable collective
bargaining agreement. The minimum PIP duration discussed in Part V applies
as a statutory floor; the collective agreement may specify a longer
duration or additional procedural protections.

## 8.4 Statutory Recordkeeping Obligations

In addition to the wage and hour recordkeeping discussed in Part III,
employers are subject to recordkeeping obligations in respect of:

(a) personnel files, including performance reviews and disciplinary records,
    typically for a minimum of three (3) years following separation;
(b) PIP documentation, mid-point review records, and outcome determinations,
    typically for a minimum of seven (7) years;
(c) anti-discrimination complaint files and investigation records, typically
    for a minimum of four (4) years;
(d) occupational safety incident records, for the periods prescribed by the
    applicable safety statute.

Failure to maintain required records does not, of itself, give rise to a
direct cause of action by an aggrieved employee, but creates evidentiary
disadvantages for the employer in any subsequent litigation.
