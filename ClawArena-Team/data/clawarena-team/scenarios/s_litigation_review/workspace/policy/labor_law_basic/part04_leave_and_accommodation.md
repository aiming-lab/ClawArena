# Part IV — Leave Entitlements and Reasonable Accommodation

## 4.1 Family and Medical Leave

Eligible employees are entitled to job-protected unpaid leave under the
applicable family and medical leave statute for specified qualifying reasons,
including the employee's own serious health condition, the care of a covered
family member with a serious health condition, the birth or placement of a
child, and certain qualifying military exigencies. Eligibility typically
requires a minimum length of service and a minimum hours-worked threshold.

## 4.2 Paid Sick Leave

A growing number of jurisdictions impose statutory paid sick leave entitlements
that apply alongside any contractual sick leave benefit. The statutory
entitlement and the contractual benefit must be administered in tandem, with
the more protective rule prevailing.

## 4.3 Pregnancy and Parental Leave

Pregnancy-related conditions are a protected ground in their own right under
applicable anti-discrimination statutes. Reasonable accommodation of
pregnancy-related limitations is required where such accommodation does not
impose undue hardship. Parental leave entitlements supplement the family and
medical leave framework and, in some jurisdictions, are paid in whole or in
part by a statutory insurance scheme.

## 4.4 Reasonable Accommodation of Disability

Employers are required to provide reasonable accommodation to qualified
employees with disabilities, where such accommodation does not impose undue
hardship. The reasonable accommodation process is interactive: the employer
must engage in good-faith dialogue with the employee to identify potential
accommodations, including modified duties, modified schedules, leave of
absence, and assistive equipment.

## 4.5 Religious Accommodation

Employers are required to provide reasonable accommodation for an employee's
sincerely held religious beliefs and practices, on a similar undue-hardship
standard. The doctrine has been recently clarified by appellate authority to
require more than de minimis cost to refuse accommodation.

## 4.6 Interaction with Performance Management

Leave taken under any of the foregoing entitlements is generally not counted
against the employee in performance management. A Performance Improvement Plan
that runs concurrent with protected leave must be extended by the duration of
the protected leave, to preserve the bona fide remediation opportunity
described in Part V.
