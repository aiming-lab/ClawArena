# Chapter 2 — Employment Classification and At-Will Status

## 2.1 At-Will Employment

Except where expressly varied by a written employment agreement signed by an
authorized officer of the Company, employment with Acme Corporation is at-will.
Either the Company or the employee may terminate the employment relationship at
any time, with or without cause and with or without prior notice, subject only
to the procedural commitments set forth in this Handbook and any obligations
imposed by applicable law.

The at-will nature of employment is not modified by oral statements,
representations of continued employment, or course of dealing. Only a written
instrument signed by the Chief Executive Officer or General Counsel may alter
the at-will relationship.

## 2.2 Employment Categories

(a) **Full-time regular** — employees scheduled to work thirty-five (35) or
    more hours per week on an ongoing basis. Full-time regular employees are
    eligible for the full benefits package described in Chapter 8.

(b) **Part-time regular** — employees scheduled to work fewer than thirty-five
    (35) but at least twenty (20) hours per week on an ongoing basis.
    Part-time regular employees are eligible for a prorated benefits package as
    described in Chapter 8.

(c) **Temporary** — employees engaged for a fixed term not exceeding twelve
    (12) months. Temporary employees are generally not eligible for benefits
    other than those required by law.

(d) **Probationary** — newly hired employees within their first ninety (90)
    days of service. Probationary employees are subject to a streamlined
    performance assessment and may be separated without recourse to the
    progressive discipline procedure set out in Chapter 6.

## 2.3 Exempt and Non-Exempt Status

Each position is classified as either exempt or non-exempt under the applicable
wage and hour laws. Classification is determined by the Human Resources
Department in consultation with Legal Affairs, based on the duties test and the
salary basis test prescribed by law. Misclassification concerns may be raised
through the grievance process set out in Chapter 7.
