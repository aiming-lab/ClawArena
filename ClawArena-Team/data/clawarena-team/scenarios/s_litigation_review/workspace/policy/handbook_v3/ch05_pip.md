# Chapter 5 — Performance Improvement Plans (PIP)

This Chapter establishes the substantive and procedural requirements applicable
to all Performance Improvement Plans issued by the Company. It is the controlling
internal authority on PIP duration, content, administration, and outcomes.

## 5.1 Purpose and Scope

A Performance Improvement Plan ("PIP") is a formal document used to address
sustained or significant performance deficiencies that have not been remediated
through informal coaching and feedback. A PIP is not a disciplinary action in and
of itself; however, failure to meet the objectives set forth in a PIP may result
in further disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment.

PIPs may be initiated at the discretion of the employee's supervisor, in
consultation with the Human Resources Department, whenever an employee's
performance is deemed to fall materially below the standards expected for their
role and grade level.

A PIP may be triggered by, without limitation:

(a) an overall annual review rating of "Does Not Meet Expectations";
(b) two consecutive annual or mid-year reviews showing a downward trend toward
    "Partially Meets Expectations" on a material competency;
(c) a documented pattern of failure to deliver against specific objectives over
    a period of not less than ninety (90) days;
(d) a critical incident — such as a material error, missed deadline of
    significant business impact, or breach of professional standards — that
    warrants formal performance attention even in the absence of (a) through
    (c) above.

In each case, the supervisor must confirm with HR that informal remediation
options have been exhausted or are inappropriate, and must document the basis
for the PIP in writing before issuing the PIP document to the employee.

## 5.2 PIP Duration — MINIMUM REQUIRED PERIOD

**The minimum duration of any Performance Improvement Plan issued to a full-time
employee of the Company is THIRTY (30) calendar days.**

This minimum period is established to afford the employee a meaningful and
reasonable opportunity to demonstrate improvement in the identified areas of
deficiency. No PIP shall be closed, and no termination decision predicated on
PIP non-completion shall be made, before the expiry of this thirty (30) day
minimum period, except in cases of gross misconduct as defined in Chapter 6.

For part-time employees working fewer than twenty (20) hours per week, the minimum
PIP duration is fifteen (15) calendar days. For probationary employees within
their first ninety (90) days of employment, no minimum PIP duration applies.

The minimum period set forth in this Section is a floor, not a ceiling. Where the
nature or complexity of the performance deficiency reasonably requires a longer
remediation period — for example, where the role involves multi-quarter project
cycles or where the deficiency relates to skills that ordinarily take more than
thirty (30) days to develop — the supervisor and HR shall set a longer initial
duration. Sixty (60) and ninety (90) day PIPs are common for senior individual
contributors and management roles.

The period runs from the date on which the employee signs the PIP document
acknowledging its terms (the "PIP commencement date") to the date on which the
PIP is formally closed (the "PIP closure date"). If the employee declines or
unreasonably delays signing, HR shall set a deemed commencement date no earlier
than five (5) business days after the PIP document was first presented to the
employee in writing.

This Section is supplemented and, where applicable, superseded by any binding
statutory or regulatory minimum that exceeds the figure stated above. Where the
applicable statutory minimum exceeds thirty (30) days, the statutory figure
governs.

## 5.3 PIP Content Requirements

Each PIP must include, at a minimum, the following elements:

(a) A clear statement of the specific performance deficiencies being addressed;
(b) Measurable improvement objectives with defined success criteria;
(c) A timeline for achieving each objective, consistent with the minimum duration
    requirements set forth in Section 5.2;
(d) Identification of the resources, training, or support to be provided by the
    Company to assist the employee in meeting the objectives;
(e) A schedule of check-in meetings, including at minimum a written mid-point
    review at the halfway mark of the PIP period;
(f) Consequences of failure to meet the stated objectives;
(g) Signatures of the employee, their direct supervisor, and an HR representative,
    confirming receipt and acknowledgement of the plan's terms.

The PIP document is the operative instrument. Oral elaborations or supplements
that conflict with the written PIP are without effect. Any modification to the
PIP during its term must be reduced to writing, agreed by the parties, and filed
with HR.

### 5.3.1 Mid-Point Review

The mid-point review required by Section 5.3(e) must be conducted in person (or
by video conference where in-person meeting is impracticable) and must produce a
written summary signed by both the supervisor and the employee. The written
summary must (i) record the employee's progress against each objective,
(ii) identify any obstacles to progress, (iii) confirm or revise the support
identified in Section 5.3(d), and (iv) state whether the employee is on track
to meet the objectives by the PIP closure date.

Failure to conduct or document the mid-point review constitutes a material
procedural defect. A termination predicated on PIP non-completion that is
preceded by a defective mid-point review is presumed procedurally unfair for
purposes of Chapter 7 and may be set aside by the Grievance Panel.

## 5.4 PIP Administration

The HR Department is responsible for maintaining records of all PIPs issued,
including signed copies of the PIP document, mid-point review notes, and final
outcome determinations. PIP records are retained for a minimum of seven (7) years
from the date of issue.

Supervisors are prohibited from communicating the existence or content of an
employee's PIP to any individual outside the HR chain of command without the
express written consent of the employee or as required by law. Where disclosure
is compelled by law or by a court of competent jurisdiction, HR shall notify
the employee in writing before disclosure is made, where lawful to do so.

The Company shall make reasonable accommodations to the PIP process where
required by applicable disability or anti-discrimination law, including, where
appropriate, extension of the PIP period to account for protected leave taken
during the PIP term.

## 5.5 PIP Outcomes

At the conclusion of the PIP period, the supervisor, in consultation with HR,
shall make one of the following determinations:

(a) **Successful completion**: The employee has met all or substantially all of
    the stated objectives. The PIP is closed and a notation to this effect is
    placed in the employee's personnel file. No further disciplinary action is
    taken based on the performance issues addressed in the PIP.

(b) **Partial completion with extension**: The employee has made measurable
    progress but has not fully met all objectives. At the supervisor's discretion,
    the PIP may be extended for an additional period not to exceed thirty (30)
    days, subject to HR approval. The extension shall be documented as an
    amendment to the original PIP and shall set out the residual objectives to
    be met during the extension period.

(c) **Failure to meet objectives**: The employee has failed to demonstrate
    adequate improvement. The supervisor may recommend further disciplinary
    action, up to and including termination, in accordance with Chapter 6 and
    Chapter 11 of this Handbook.

A determination under this Section must be communicated to the employee in
writing within five (5) business days of the PIP closure date. The
communication must state the determination, the supporting reasons, and the
employee's right to seek review under Chapter 7.

## 5.6 Appeals

An employee who disagrees with a PIP outcome determination, or who contends that
the PIP process was procedurally defective, may appeal under the procedure set
out in Chapter 7. An appeal does not stay any termination decision, but a
successful appeal may result in reinstatement, expungement of the PIP record,
or such other remedy as the Grievance Panel deems appropriate.
