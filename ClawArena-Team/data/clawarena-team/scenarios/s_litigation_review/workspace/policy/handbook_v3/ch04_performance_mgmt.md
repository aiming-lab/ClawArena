# Chapter 4 — Performance Management Framework

The Company employs a structured performance management cycle designed to support
employee development, recognize high performance, and address performance
deficiencies in a fair and transparent manner. The framework described in this
Chapter is the predecessor and complement to the formal Performance Improvement
Plan procedure set out in Chapter 5.

## 4.1 Annual Performance Review

All regular full-time and part-time employees are subject to an annual performance
review conducted by their direct supervisor, in consultation with the relevant
department head. Reviews are typically conducted in December for the preceding
calendar year and form the basis for compensation adjustments effective the
following February.

Performance is evaluated against pre-established objectives, competencies, and
behavioral indicators. Employees receive a written summary of their performance
rating no later than thirty (30) days after the review meeting. Employees may
submit a written response, which is filed with the review record.

The standard rating scale comprises five levels: Exceeds Expectations, Meets
Expectations, Meets Most Expectations, Partially Meets Expectations, and Does
Not Meet Expectations. An overall rating of "Does Not Meet Expectations" — or,
in some circumstances, "Partially Meets Expectations" combined with a specific
deficiency — is a customary basis for initiation of the Chapter 5 PIP process.

## 4.2 Mid-Year Check-In

In addition to the annual review, supervisors are required to conduct at least one
formal mid-year check-in with each direct report. The purpose of this check-in is
to assess progress against annual objectives, identify any emerging performance
concerns, and provide developmental feedback. Mid-year check-ins are documented
in the Company's Human Resources Information System (HRIS).

The mid-year check-in is not a disciplinary instrument; however, where the
check-in identifies a material performance deficiency, the supervisor must
provide the employee with a written summary of the concern within five (5)
business days and offer a reasonable opportunity for remediation. Sustained
failure to address such concerns may, in the supervisor's discretion and in
consultation with HR, lead to initiation of a PIP under Chapter 5.

## 4.3 Continuous Feedback Culture

The Company encourages a culture of continuous feedback in which supervisors and
employees engage in regular informal performance conversations. Such conversations
do not replace formal reviews but supplement them. Documentation of informal
feedback is strongly encouraged but not required unless the feedback relates to a
potential disciplinary matter, in which case Section 4.2 documentation
requirements apply.

## 4.4 Relationship to PIPs

A Performance Improvement Plan under Chapter 5 is reserved for sustained or
significant performance deficiencies that have not been remediated through the
mechanisms described in this Chapter. Supervisors should not initiate a PIP
without first having attempted informal coaching and feedback, except where the
performance deficiency is of such severity that immediate formalization is
warranted and HR has so authorized.
