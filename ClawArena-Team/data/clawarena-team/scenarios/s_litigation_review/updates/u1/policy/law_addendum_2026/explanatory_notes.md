# Labor Standards Addendum 2026 — Explanatory Notes

*These notes are non-binding regulatory commentary issued by the Division of
Employment Standards to assist employers, employees, and adjudicators in
interpreting the Addendum. In the event of any inconsistency between these
notes and the operative articles, the operative articles prevail.*

## 1. Purpose and Origin

The Division undertook a review of the prior thirty (30) day minimum PIP
duration in response to a sustained pattern of complaints from affected
employees that thirty days is, in many roles, an inadequate period in which
to receive feedback, internalize the required behavioral changes, and
demonstrate improvement. The forty-five (45) day figure adopted in Article II
Section 2.1 reflects the Division's assessment of (i) the minimum period
required for a bona fide remediation opportunity in a typical knowledge-work
role, (ii) the time required to conduct a meaningful mid-point review, and
(iii) the time required to collect and assess evidence of improvement on the
employee's part.

## 2. Retroactivity

The retroactive applicability of the Addendum is unusual but is justified by
the corrective character of the measure. Employers that took adverse
employment actions in the window between the effective date and the
publication date are exposed to retroactive enforcement under Article VI
Section 6.5. The Division will exercise its enforcement discretion to focus
on cases where the employer's non-compliance is material and where the
affected employee has suffered real prejudice.

## 3. Interaction with Internal Handbooks

The minimum durations established by Article II are a regulatory floor.
Where an employer's handbook specifies a longer minimum duration, the
handbook governs. Where an employer's handbook specifies a shorter
duration, the handbook is deemed amended by operation of Article VI
Section 6.6, and the employer is required to formally update the handbook
within sixty (60) days under Article V Section 5.1(a).

## 4. Mid-Point Review

The Division emphasizes that the mid-point review required by Article III is
a substantive procedural protection and not a formality. A perfunctory
mid-point review — for example, one that does not address each PIP objective
or that does not record any obstacles identified by the employee — does not
satisfy Article III and exposes the employer to the consequences set out in
Section 3.1.

## 5. Documentation

Employers are reminded that the Division and reviewing courts will draw
adverse inferences from the absence of contemporaneous documentation. Where
the employer cannot produce a signed PIP document with a clear commencement
date, the Division will treat the PIP as having commenced on the latest
date for which there is evidentiary support, which will frequently
disadvantage the employer's defense.

## 6. Enforcement Priorities

In the first year following the effective date, the Division will prioritize
enforcement against (i) terminations effected before the expiry of the
forty-five (45) day minimum, (ii) absence of mid-point review documentation,
and (iii) employer failure to provide the notice to employees required by
Article V Section 5.2.

## 7. Resources

Employers requiring assistance with implementation may consult the
Division's published implementation guide and may request informal advice
from the Division's compliance assistance team. Such requests are
confidential and do not constitute waiver of any privilege.
