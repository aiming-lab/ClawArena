## Helios Cloud (March 2019 – August 2022)

My three and a half years at Helios Cloud were formative in establishing my approach to
platform reliability. I joined as a mid-level software engineer and was promoted to Staff
Software Engineer after 18 months, following the successful delivery of the observability
migration project.

The observability migration was the defining project of my time at Helios. The legacy
Nagios/Graphite stack had become a bottleneck — alert fatigue was high, coverage was
incomplete, and the team was spending more time debugging the monitoring infrastructure
than the services it was supposed to monitor. I proposed, scoped, and led the migration
to Prometheus/Grafana/Thanos over 14 months. The project involved coordinating with 12
service teams, rewriting alert rules, and training the engineering organization on the
new tooling.

The results were measurable: alert signal-to-noise improved dramatically, the team
reduced false positive pages by approximately 60%, and we were able to define and
instrument formal SLOs for all Tier-1 services for the first time in the company's
history. I'm proud that the architecture I designed is still in use today.

I departed Helios Cloud in August 2022 to seek a role with a larger scope and more
direct ownership of infrastructure decisions. Helios was a strong team but had reached
a maturity plateau; I wanted to be part of building out a platform at an earlier stage
of scale. I left on very good terms and maintained professional relationships with
several colleagues, two of whom have agreed to serve as references for this application.

My GitHub handle at Helios was jreyes-eng, which I have maintained as my primary
public engineering identity. The handle has activity from my Helios tenure onward.

## Stratum Labs (September 2022 – November 2025)

At Stratum, I joined as a Senior Infrastructure Engineer with a mandate to redesign
the deployment pipeline and improve the reliability posture of the production environment.
The engineering org had scaled quickly and the deployment infrastructure had not kept pace;
deployments were slow, brittle, and frequently required manual intervention.

I led the Kubernetes deployment pipeline rebuild over approximately 9 months, cutting
deployment lead time from 47 minutes to under 8 minutes. More importantly, I made the
pipeline fail-safe: automated rollback on failure reduced MTTR from 23 minutes to 6
minutes and largely eliminated the need for middle-of-the-night manual recovery operations.

I also contributed significantly to the chaos engineering program, which was entirely
new to Stratum when I arrived. Introducing chaos engineering required careful change
management — it was a cultural shift as much as a technical one — and I worked closely
with engineering leadership to build buy-in and set appropriate expectations.

I am leaving Stratum in November 2025 to seek a Staff-level title with broader
organizational scope. I am proud of the foundation I helped build at Stratum and believe
the platform is in a strong state to support the next phase of the company's growth.

## Why Meridian Systems

Meridian's platform reliability team operates at a scale and with a level of engineering
rigor that I find genuinely compelling. The emphasis on SLO-driven reliability, investment
in observability tooling, and the expectation that Staff Engineers own both the technical
direction and the organizational narrative of their domain are exactly the things I have
been working toward.

I am confident that my experience across both the Helios and Stratum tenures — and
particularly the depth of ownership I have had over observability stacks, deployment
pipelines, and incident response processes — positions me well for the role.
