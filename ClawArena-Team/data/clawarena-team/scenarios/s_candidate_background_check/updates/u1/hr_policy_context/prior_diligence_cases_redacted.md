at Meridian Systems. All candidate names, roles, and employer names have been redacted
or anonymized. Cases are included for calibration purposes only.

These cases are background reference material. None of the cases involve the Jordan
Reyes candidacy or any current candidate.

Section 5.1 — PIP Disclosure Requirement. Candidates are required to self-disclose any active or recently concluded performance improvement plan (PIP) or formal disciplinary action initiated within the 24 months preceding the application date. Failure to self-disclose a PIP that is subsequently revealed through the reference interview process constitutes a material omission and triggers an automatic risk tier upgrade to High per Section 6.3.

Under California Labor Code and similar state frameworks, employers are generally prohibited from asking references to disclose information about a former employee's disciplinary history beyond dates of employment and position held, unless the reference contact volunteers the information independently. Where a reference contact voluntarily discloses PIP history, the employing company's receipt and use of that information is generally permissible for diligence purposes.

Performance improvement plans (PIPs) are internal management tools used by employers to document performance concerns and establish measurable improvement objectives for employees who are not meeting expectations. The existence of a PIP is generally treated as a confidential internal employment matter. However, where a candidate is asked to self-disclose recent PIPs as part of a background diligence process, failure to disclose a material PIP constitutes a misrepresentation under most employment law frameworks.

However, employers remain subject to applicable state employment verification laws, anti-discrimination statutes, and the implied covenant of fair dealing.
