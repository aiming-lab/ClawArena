## I. Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors

### Criteria for Authorship

The ICMJE recommends that authorship be based on the following four criteria:

1. **Substantial contributions** to the conception or design of the work; or the acquisition,
   analysis, or interpretation of data for the work; AND

2. **Drafting the work or revising it critically** for important intellectual content; AND

3. **Final approval** of the version to be published; AND

4. **Agreement to be accountable** for all aspects of the work in ensuring that questions related
   to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated and
   resolved.

In addition to being accountable for the parts of the work they have done, authors should be
able to identify which co-authors are responsible for other specific parts of the work. In
addition, authors should have confidence in the integrity of the contributions of their
co-authors.

All those designated as authors should meet all four criteria for authorship, and all who meet
the four criteria should be identified as authors. Those who do not meet all four criteria
should be acknowledged.

### First Authorship

First authorship is conventionally assigned to the investigator who made the primary intellectual
contribution to the work — typically the researcher who conceived the central scientific
question, designed the principal analytical approach, and led the execution of the research.
In computational and analytical studies, the first author is typically the investigator who
designed and implemented the core methodology. Commit counts in version-controlled repositories
may serve as one proxy measure of intellectual contribution but should not be used in isolation.

### Corresponding Authorship

The corresponding author is the individual who takes responsibility for communicating with the
journal during manuscript preparation and review, and for responding to post-publication
correspondence. The corresponding author should be the investigator who takes primary
accountability for the work as a whole. Institutional considerations, including IRB sponsorship,
may be relevant but are not determinative in isolation.
