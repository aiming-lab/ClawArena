## Article I — Incorporation and Registered Office

### §1.1 — Corporate Name

The corporation is incorporated under the laws of the Province of Ontario and
the Canada Business Corporations Act (CBCA) under the name **Meridian Capital
Holdings Inc.** (hereinafter "the Corporation" or "Meridian Capital").

### §1.2 — Registered Office

The registered office of the Corporation is located at 200 Bay Street, Suite 3100,
Toronto, Ontario, M5J 2W7, or such other address as the board of directors may
from time to time determine by resolution.

### §1.3 — Fiscal Year

The fiscal year of the Corporation commences on January 1 of each calendar year
and ends on December 31 of the same calendar year, unless otherwise determined
by the board.

### §1.4 — Corporate Seal

The Corporation may, but is not required to, maintain a corporate seal. The
absence of a seal does not affect the validity of any instrument executed on
behalf of the Corporation.



## Article II — Objects and Purposes

### §2.1 — Principal Objects

The Corporation is incorporated for the following principal objects:

(a) To carry on the business of a financial holding company, including the
    ownership and management of interests in financial services entities;
(b) To invest in and manage portfolios of securities, loans, and other financial
    assets for the Corporation's own account;
(c) To provide financial, strategic, and operational advisory services to
    affiliated and third-party entities;
(d) To engage in any other lawful activity incidental or ancillary to the
    foregoing principal objects.

### §2.2 — Ancillary Powers

In furtherance of its objects, the Corporation shall have all the powers
conferred by the Canada Business Corporations Act and its incorporating
documents, including but not limited to the power to borrow money, issue
securities, grant security interests, and deal generally in property.



## Article III — Capital Structure and Share Classes

### §3.1 — Authorised Capital

The Corporation is authorised to issue an unlimited number of Common Shares and
an unlimited number of Preferred Shares, issuable in series, subject to the
restrictions and terms set out in this Article.

### §3.2 — Common Shares

Each Common Share carries one vote at shareholder meetings, subject to applicable
law, and entitles the holder to receive dividends as declared by the board and to
participate in the assets of the Corporation upon liquidation.

### §3.3 — Preferred Shares

The board is authorised by resolution, and without shareholder approval, to fix
the terms, rights, privileges, restrictions, and conditions attaching to each
series of Preferred Shares before issue. Such terms may include cumulative or
non-cumulative dividends, redemption provisions, conversion rights, and voting
rights on such matters and in such circumstances as the board determines.

### §3.4 — Listing and Trading

Common Shares of the Corporation are listed and posted for trading on the Toronto
Stock Exchange (TSX) under the symbol MCI. The Corporation is subject to all
requirements of the TSX Company Manual and applicable Canadian securities
legislation as a listed issuer.



## Article IV — Director Composition and Qualification

### §4.1 — Board Size

The board of directors of the Corporation shall consist of not fewer than five (5)
and not more than eleven (11) directors, as determined from time to time by
ordinary resolution of shareholders.

The current board composition is seven (7) directors, including the Board Chair.

### §4.2 — Director Qualification

A person is qualified to be a director of the Corporation if they:

(a) Are at least eighteen (18) years of age;
(b) Are not an undischarged bankrupt;
(c) Are not of unsound mind as determined by a court;
(d) Satisfy the applicable independence and residency requirements under the
    Canada Business Corporations Act and any applicable securities legislation;
(e) Have disclosed all interests that may give rise to a conflict of interest
    as required by Article IV §4.5 and Article V §5.2 of this Charter.

### §4.3 — Director Independence

A majority of directors must be independent within the meaning of National
Instrument 52-110 (Audit Committees) and the TSX Company Manual. The Board Chair
and all committee chairs must be independent directors.

### §4.4 — Director Terms

Directors are elected annually by shareholders at the annual general meeting.
Each director serves a one-year term and is eligible for re-election. The board
may appoint directors to fill vacancies between annual meetings; such appointments
are effective until the next annual general meeting.

### §4.5 — Director Disclosure Obligations

Each director shall, on or before their first board meeting after appointment or
election, submit a completed conflict-of-interest disclosure form as prescribed by
the Board Conflict of Interest Policy. Directors shall update their disclosure
annually and promptly upon becoming aware of any new interest requiring disclosure.



## Article V — Board Meetings and Agenda Procedures

### §5.1 — Regular Board Meetings

The board of directors shall hold at least four (4) regular meetings per calendar
year, scheduled at the commencement of each calendar year and communicated to all
directors no later than December 1 of the preceding year. Regular meetings shall
be held at times and locations determined by the Board Chair in consultation with
the Corporate Secretary.

A quorum for the transaction of business at a regular board meeting shall be a
majority of the then-incumbent directors.

### §5.2 — Agenda Preparation and Circulation

The Corporate Secretary is responsible for preparing, in consultation with the
Board Chair and the Chief Executive Officer, a draft agenda for each regular
board meeting. The draft agenda must be circulated to all directors no later than
five (5) business days before the scheduled meeting date, together with all
supporting materials required for the items on the agenda.

Directors may submit requests for additional agenda items no later than ten (10)
business days before the meeting date, subject to the Chair's approval and the
priority rules set out in §5.3.

### §5.3 — Standing Agenda Order

**The standing agenda order for all regular board meetings of Meridian Capital
Holdings shall be as follows:**

**(1) Call to Order**
**(2) Conflicts of Interest Disclosures**
**(3) Approval of Previous Minutes**
**(4) Financial Review**
**(5) Strategic Items**
**(6) Regulatory & Compliance**
**(7) Any Other Business**

No standing item shall be moved, merged, or omitted without a two-thirds majority
vote of directors present at the meeting, except as expressly provided in
Article VI of this Charter.

The ordering of items under §5.3 reflects the board's priority obligations:
directors must first declare conflicts before deliberating on any substantive
matter; financial oversight precedes strategic deliberation; and regulatory and
compliance items are held separately from strategic items to ensure they receive
adequate dedicated attention.

### §5.4 — Voting Procedures

Resolutions of the board are passed by a majority of directors present and voting,
unless this Charter or applicable law requires a higher threshold. In the event of
an equality of votes, the Chair does not have a casting vote; the resolution is
defeated.

### §5.5 — Minutes and Records

The Corporate Secretary is responsible for preparing minutes of each board meeting
promptly after the meeting, typically within fifteen (15) business days. Minutes
must be circulated to all directors for review and approved at the next meeting.



## Article VI — Emergency and Urgent Agenda Items

### §6.1 — General Principle

Notwithstanding the standing agenda order established in Article V §5.3, the
board recognises that urgent matters may occasionally arise that require board
consideration at a scheduled meeting on an expedited basis. This Article
establishes the procedures governing the insertion of such items into the
board's agenda.

### §6.2 — Chief Executive Officer Emergency Items

Emergency items submitted by the Chief Executive Officer within ten business days of a scheduled meeting shall be placed immediately following the Conflicts of Interest Disclosures item (position 2a) and shall not displace standing items 3 through 7.

The position designated as "2a" is immediately following the Conflicts of Interest
Disclosures item (position 2) and immediately preceding the Approval of Previous
Minutes (position 3). It does not constitute a new standing item and applies only
to the specific meeting at which the emergency item is inserted.

The Corporate Secretary shall confirm in writing to the Chief Executive Officer,
no later than two (2) business days after receipt of the request, whether the item
satisfies the conditions of this section and its placement in the revised agenda.

### §6.3 — Other Director Emergency Items

Any director (other than the Chief Executive Officer) seeking to insert an urgent
item into a scheduled agenda must submit a written request to the Board Chair and
the Corporate Secretary no later than five (5) business days before the meeting.
The Board Chair shall have sole discretion to approve or reject such a request,
having regard to the urgency of the matter and the available meeting time.

Emergency items approved under this section shall be placed immediately before
Any Other Business (position 7), not in the position 2a reserved for CEO items.

### §6.4 — Excluded Matters

The following matters shall not be designated as emergency agenda items under
this Article:

(a) Matters that could have been raised as regular agenda items within the
    ordinary ten-business-day submission window;
(b) Matters that the submitting director acknowledges require more than thirty
    (30) minutes of substantive board discussion, which must be scheduled as
    a full agenda item at the next regular meeting or at a special meeting;
(c) Matters that are the subject of pending litigation or regulatory investigation,
    unless General Counsel has confirmed in writing that immediate board
    consideration is required.



## Article VII — Committees of the Board

### §7.1 — Standing Committees

The board shall maintain the following standing committees:

(a) **Audit Committee** — comprising not fewer than three independent directors;
    responsible for oversight of financial reporting, internal controls, external
    audit, and risk management.
(b) **Human Resources and Compensation Committee** — comprising not fewer than
    three independent directors; responsible for executive compensation, succession
    planning, and board evaluation.
(c) **Governance and Nominating Committee** — comprising not fewer than three
    independent directors; responsible for board composition, director orientation,
    governance policy, and charter compliance.
(d) **Risk Committee** — comprising not fewer than three directors; responsible
    for enterprise risk oversight, including financial, operational, and regulatory
    risk.

### §7.2 — Committee Charters

Each standing committee shall have a written charter approved by the full board.
Committee charters shall be reviewed annually by the Governance and Nominating
Committee and amended as required. Current committee charters are available on
the corporate intranet and in the corporate governance section of the annual
proxy circular.

### §7.3 — Committee Reporting

Each committee shall report to the full board at every regular meeting on its
activities since the prior meeting. Committee reports shall be included in the
board meeting materials distributed by the Corporate Secretary and shall be
presented as part of the relevant standing agenda item (e.g., Audit Committee
report under Financial Review; Governance Committee report under Strategic Items).



## Article VIII — Officers and Delegation

### §8.1 — Officers

The officers of the Corporation shall include at minimum:

- Chief Executive Officer
- Chief Financial Officer
- Corporate Secretary
- General Counsel

Additional officers may be appointed by the board as it sees fit.

### §8.2 — Chief Executive Officer

The Chief Executive Officer is responsible for the general management and
supervision of the business and affairs of the Corporation, subject to the
direction of the board of directors. The CEO reports to the board and attends
all board meetings unless the board or a committee thereof resolves to meet
in camera without the CEO present.

### §8.3 — Corporate Secretary

The Corporate Secretary is responsible for:

(a) Maintaining the corporate records and minute books of the Corporation;
(b) Preparing and distributing board and committee meeting materials;
(c) Giving notice of, and attending, all meetings of the board and shareholders;
(d) Maintaining the register of directors, officers, and shareholders;
(e) Filing all required documents with regulatory authorities and the stock exchange;
(f) Advising the board on corporate governance matters and charter compliance.

### §8.4 — General Counsel

The General Counsel is responsible for overseeing the legal affairs of the
Corporation, providing legal advice to the board and management, managing
external legal relationships, and ensuring compliance with applicable law
and regulatory requirements. The General Counsel attends board meetings as
required and advises the Corporate Secretary on charter interpretation.



## Article IX — Indemnification and Insurance

### §9.1 — Indemnification of Directors and Officers

Subject to applicable law, the Corporation shall indemnify each current and
former director and officer of the Corporation (and of its subsidiaries, where
applicable) against all costs, charges, and expenses, including legal fees,
reasonably incurred by that person in connection with any civil, criminal,
administrative, investigative, or other proceeding in which they are a party
or are threatened to be made a party by reason of being or having been a
director or officer of the Corporation.

Indemnification is conditional on the person having acted honestly and in good
faith with a view to the best interests of the Corporation, and, in the case of
a criminal or administrative action or proceeding, having had reasonable grounds
for believing their conduct was lawful.

### §9.2 — Directors' and Officers' Liability Insurance

The Corporation shall maintain, at its expense, directors' and officers' liability
insurance in an amount and with such terms and conditions as the board considers
appropriate, having regard to the Corporation's size, activities, and risk profile.



## Article X — Amendments to the Charter

### §10.1 — Amendment Procedure

This Charter may be amended by a special resolution of shareholders, being a
resolution passed by at least two-thirds of the votes cast at a meeting of
shareholders duly called for that purpose, subject to any additional requirements
under the Canada Business Corporations Act.

The board may amend the Charter without shareholder approval only to the extent
permitted by the CBCA, which includes certain administrative matters such as
correcting typographical errors and updating the registered office address.

### §10.2 — Bylaw Annotations

The General Counsel shall maintain a bylaw annotation memo explaining the
rationale for significant changes made in each charter amendment. The current
annotation memo, covering the v3.1 → v3.2 amendments, is available at
`charter_docs/bylaw_annotation_memo.md`.

### §10.3 — Version Control

The Corporate Secretary is responsible for maintaining a complete version history
of all charter amendments. Each version shall be identified by a version number,
an effective date, and the authorising board resolution number. Superseded versions
shall be archived in accordance with the Corporation's document retention policy.
