## Part B — Outside Interests Disclosure

I, Sofia Alba, hereby disclose the following outside interests that may be
relevant to my duties as a director of Meridian Capital Holdings:

### Interest 1: Nexus Fintech Partners LP

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Entity Name | Nexus Fintech Partners LP |
| Nature of Interest | Limited partnership unit holding (investment) |
| Percentage of Interest | Not determinative — see rationale |
| Date of Acquisition | 2024-08-15 |
| Relationship to Meridian Capital | Potentially material — see rationale |

**Classification:** POTENTIAL MATERIAL CONFLICT REQUIRING FULL BOARD VOTE

**Rationale for Material Classification:**

While my LP unit holding in Nexus Fintech Partners LP is a minority interest,
I have recently become aware of a structural fact that I believe elevates this
interest to a potentially material conflict.

**Key fact:** Nexus Fintech Partners LP holds warrants to acquire shares in
Meridian Capital Fintech Solutions Ltd., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Meridian
Capital Holdings. The warrants, if exercised, would give Nexus Fintech Partners LP
a 4.8% equity interest in Meridian Capital Fintech Solutions Ltd. As an LP
unitholder, I hold an indirect economic interest in those warrants.

This means that any decision by the Meridian Capital board that affects the
value of Meridian Capital Fintech Solutions Ltd. — including any acquisition,
disposition, financing, or restructuring of that subsidiary — would have a
direct indirect effect on the value of my LP units through the warrants.

I have reviewed the Board COI Policy materiality threshold guidance and, in my
judgment, the indirect warrant exposure through the LP makes this interest
potentially material. I am therefore classifying it as a potential material
conflict requiring full board vote to resolve the classification.

## Part C — Comparison with Other Disclosures

I am aware that at least one other director has disclosed an interest in Nexus
Fintech Partners LP and has classified it as non-material. My classification
differs. I have applied the more conservative assessment in light of the
warrant structure described above, which I believe the other director's
disclosure may not have fully addressed.

In accordance with the Board COI Policy, where two directors classify the same
interest differently, the matter should be resolved by the Corporate Secretary
in consultation with General Counsel, and if necessary by board vote.
