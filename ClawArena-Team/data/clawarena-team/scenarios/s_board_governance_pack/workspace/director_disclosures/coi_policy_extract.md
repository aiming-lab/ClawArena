# Board Conflict of Interest Policy — Extract
# Meridian Capital Holdings
# Version: 2.3 (Effective: 2024-01-01)
# Source: Full policy available from Corporate Secretary's office

---

## Section 1 — Purpose

This policy establishes the framework for identifying, disclosing, and managing
conflicts of interest involving directors and officers of Meridian Capital Holdings
and its subsidiaries. The policy is designed to protect the integrity of board
decision-making and to ensure compliance with applicable securities legislation
and corporate governance best practices.

---

## Section 2 — Definitions

**"Conflict of Interest"** means any situation in which a director or officer has
a personal interest — financial, family, professional, or otherwise — that could
reasonably be expected to impair their ability to act in the best interests of the
Corporation, or that creates or could create the appearance of such impairment.

**"Material Interest"** means an interest where the personal benefit or detriment
to the director or officer, or the indirect benefit or detriment through an
affiliated entity, is reasonably likely to be significant in the context of the
matter before the board. The following are indicative (not exhaustive) indicia
of materiality:

(a) The director or officer holds more than 5% of the equity or voting rights
    in an entity that is a party to, or has a material stake in, a matter before
    the board;
(b) The director or officer receives compensation from, or has an advisory role
    in, an entity that is a party to a matter before the board;
(c) The director or officer has a direct or indirect financial exposure through
    derivative instruments, warrants, or convertible securities in an entity
    affected by a matter before the board;
(d) The director or officer has a family relationship with a person who has a
    material financial interest in a matter before the board.

**"Non-Material Interest"** means an interest that does not meet the material
interest threshold, taking into account all relevant circumstances.

---

## Section 3 — Disclosure Requirements

All directors and officers must disclose any actual or potential conflict of
interest, whether material or non-material, at the earliest practicable
opportunity. Disclosure must be made:

(a) On the prescribed COI disclosure form, submitted to the Corporate Secretary;
(b) Verbally at the relevant board meeting before the conflicted item is discussed;
(c) In writing to the Corporate Secretary within two business days of becoming
    aware of any new conflict arising after the submission of the annual form.

---

## Section 4 — Classification and Governing Rule

### §4.1 — Self-Classification

A director making an initial disclosure shall classify their interest as either
"material" or "non-material" on the prescribed form, providing the factual basis
for their classification.

### §4.2 — Review by Corporate Secretary

The Corporate Secretary shall review all submitted disclosure forms and, in
consultation with General Counsel, may revise the classification if the stated
rationale does not support the director's self-classification under the policy's
materiality framework.

### §4.3 — Conflicting Classifications Between Directors

When two or more directors disclose an interest in the same entity or the same
external arrangement but classify that interest differently, the following rule
applies:

**Governing rule:**

Where two or more directors submit conflicting classifications of the same interest, the more conservative classification shall govern for all agenda and disclosure purposes until the board resolves the matter by majority vote.

This rule reflects the precautionary principle in corporate governance: where
there is reasonable disagreement about whether an interest is material, the safer
course is to treat it as material and subject it to board scrutiny, rather than
allowing it to pass unexamined on the basis of one director's more permissive
self-classification.

### §4.4 — Consequences of Material Classification

Where an interest is classified as material (whether by self-classification or
by application of §4.3's governing rule):

(a) The director must recuse themselves from any board discussion and vote on
    any matter in which the material interest arises;
(b) The interest must be identified as a sub-item under the COI Disclosures
    agenda item, flagged for board attention;
(c) The board must, at the meeting at which the conflict is first raised, resolve
    by majority vote whether the director may continue to participate in
    discussions on the relevant matter or whether full recusal is required;
(d) The Corporate Secretary must record the board's resolution in the minutes.

---

## Section 5 — Recusal Procedures

### §5.1 — Scope of Recusal

A director who has declared a material conflict of interest in respect of a
particular agenda item shall:

(a) Absent themselves from the meeting room (or disconnect from the video
    conference) during deliberation on that item;
(b) Not receive any materials or communications relating exclusively to that
    item (other than the disclosure notice itself);
(c) Not vote on any resolution relating to that item.

### §5.2 — Record of Recusal

The Corporate Secretary shall note in the minutes: (a) the name of the director
who recused themselves; (b) the item(s) from which they recused; (c) the time
of departure and return (where applicable); and (d) the board's resolution under
§4.4(c) if applicable.

---

## Section 6 — Post-Meeting Obligations

Directors who have disclosed a material conflict in a prior period must re-certify
annually that the conflict has not changed or, if it has, file an updated
disclosure form describing the change. Failure to re-certify is treated as an
implied representation that the prior disclosure remains current.
