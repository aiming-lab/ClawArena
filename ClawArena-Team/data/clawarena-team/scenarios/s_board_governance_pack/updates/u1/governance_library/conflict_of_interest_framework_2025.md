# Conflict of Interest Classification Framework — Financial Services Boards 2025
# Synthesised Reference Document
# Background for COI materiality threshold analysis

---

## Overview

This framework document addresses the classification of director conflicts of
interest in financial services boards, with particular attention to indirect
interests arising through investment fund structures, limited partnerships,
and warrant arrangements.

---

## Part I — Materiality Standard

The materiality standard for COI classification in Canadian governance is
objective: whether a reasonable director, fully informed of the facts, would
recognise the interest as material. Self-classification is the starting point
but is not determinative where reasonable people could reach different conclusions.

### Indirect Interests Through Investment Fund Structures

A director who holds units in a limited partnership fund that in turn holds
warrants or options in a company affiliated with the director's board employer
has an indirect financial interest in the outcome of decisions affecting that
affiliated company. Whether this interest is material depends on:
(a) The size of the director's LP interest;
(b) The size of the fund's warrant position relative to the affiliated company;
(c) The nature of the decisions likely to come before the board;
(d) The director's ability to influence the relevant decisions.

### Governing Classification Rule

Where two directors reach different conclusions on the materiality of the same
interest, the board's COI policy should specify which classification governs.
Best practice, as reflected in most Canadian corporate governance policies, is
that the more conservative (material) classification governs pending board
resolution. This reflects the precautionary principle in corporate governance.
