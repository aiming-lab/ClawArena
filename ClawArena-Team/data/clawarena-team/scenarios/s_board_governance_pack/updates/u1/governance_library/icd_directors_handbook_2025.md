## Foreword

The ICD Directors Handbook provides practical guidance for directors serving on
Canadian boards, with emphasis on governance best practices, fiduciary duties,
and effective board administration. This edition incorporates updates reflecting
regulatory developments in 2024-2025.

---

## Chapter 1 — Director Duties and Responsibilities

Directors of Canadian corporations are subject to fiduciary obligations under
the Canada Business Corporations Act and applicable common law. These duties
require directors to act honestly, in good faith, and with a view to the best
interests of the corporation.

### 1.1 — Duty of Care

Directors must exercise the care, diligence, and skill that a reasonably prudent
person would exercise in comparable circumstances. This standard is objective and
requires directors to be adequately informed before making decisions.

### 1.2 — Fiduciary Duty

Directors owe a fiduciary duty to the corporation (not to individual shareholders
or other stakeholders, except where statute requires). The fiduciary duty prohibits
directors from: (a) placing personal interests above corporate interests;
(b) exploiting corporate opportunities for personal gain; (c) acting in a
conflicted manner without disclosure and appropriate recusal.

## Chapter 2 — Emergency Agenda Items

### 2.1 — Rationale for Emergency Procedures

Boards occasionally face situations where urgent matters require consideration
at a scheduled meeting without having been included in the regular agenda
preparation process. The ICD recommends that boards have clear procedures for
accommodating such matters without compromising the standing agenda structure.

### 2.2 — ICD Recommended Practice

The ICD recommends that boards establish a clear rule for emergency agenda item
placement. The rule should:
(a) Specify who may submit emergency items (typically only senior officers);
(b) Set a submission window (e.g., within a specified number of business days);
(c) Specify the placement position (e.g., immediately after conflicts disclosures);
(d) Confirm that emergency items do not displace standing items.

The ICD's recommended placement for emergency items is immediately following
the conflicts of interest disclosure item, to ensure that any director conflicts
relating to the emergency item are disclosed before deliberation commences.
