## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes NovaTherx Biosciences'
requirements for maintaining the integrity of electronic clinical trial data,
with particular emphasis on the hierarchy of authoritative data sources and
the procedures for reconciling discrepancies between primary and secondary
data records.

This SOP applies to all NovaTherx-sponsored clinical trials and to all
contracted parties (including CROs, investigator sites, and data management
vendors) operating under a Master Services Agreement with NovaTherx.

---

## 2. Definitions

**Electronic Data Capture (EDC) System:** The validated electronic system used
by investigator site staff to enter and transmit clinical trial data, including
adverse event records, to the sponsor's database.

**CRO Consolidated Summary:** Any tabulation, summary table, or narrative
document prepared by a contracted CRO that aggregates or derives data from the
EDC records. CRO consolidated summaries are secondary documents and are
subordinate to the EDC records in the authoritative hierarchy established by
this SOP.

**Primary Source Document:** The original record of any data point in the
clinical trial, including investigator site records, laboratory reports, and
medical records. Primary source documents take precedence over all electronic
and derived records.

**Data Clarification Form (DCF):** A formal document used to query, correct,
or supplement a specific data point in the EDC after the data lock, subject
to the approval of the principal investigator.

## 3. AUTHORITATIVE DATA SOURCE HIERARCHY

### 3.1 Hierarchy — Binding on All NovaTherx Sponsored Studies

For all adverse event data, including serious adverse event classification,
grade determination, and onset and resolution dates, the following hierarchy
of authoritative data sources applies:

**Tier 1 (Highest Authority):** Primary source documents and investigator
medical records. Where a treating physician has documented their clinical
assessment of an adverse event grade in the medical record, that assessment
takes precedence over all subsequent administrative classifications.

**Tier 2 (High Authority):** Electronic Data Capture (EDC) system records,
as entered by trained investigator site staff from primary source documents.
**The EDC is the authoritative source for all adverse event data submitted
to the sponsor and to regulatory authorities.** EDC records supersede any
derived, summarised, or aggregated data produced from those records.

**Tier 3 (Supporting):** CRO-generated consolidated summary tables, narrative
safety summaries, and other derived documents. These documents are produced
from EDC data and serve as working summaries for sponsor review. **CRO
consolidated summaries are not authoritative and must be verified against the
EDC before use in any regulatory submission.**

**Tier 4 (Informational only):** Draft documents, pre-approval narrative
summaries, and any document explicitly marked as WITHDRAWN or SUPERSEDED.
Such documents must not be cited in any regulatory submission or audit
deliverable. In particular, any document labelled "DRAFT", "v1 DRAFT",
"WITHDRAWN", or "SUPERSEDED" must be treated as non-authoritative.

### 3.2 Practical Application

When performing a SAE reconciliation (such as the type described in SOP-DI-003-v3
Section 4.5), the audit team must:

(a) Extract Grade-3 SAE counts from the EDC export CSV files for each site;
(b) Compare these EDC-derived counts against the CRO's consolidated summary;
(c) Identify and document any discrepancies;
(d) Resolve classification ambiguities by reference to the treating physician's
    documentation in the EDC and any relevant query resolution correspondence;
(e) Report the EDC-based counts as the authoritative figures in all deliverables.

The CRO's figures may be referenced for comparison purposes but must not be
adopted as the correct figures without verification against the EDC.

## 4. SAE Reconciliation Procedures

### 4.1 Routine Reconciliation

Routine SAE reconciliations are performed at each scheduled Data Monitoring
Committee (DMC) meeting and at the time of data lock. The regulatory affairs
team is responsible for comparing EDC-based SAE counts against the CRO's
consolidated summary and documenting any discrepancies in the study master file.

### 4.2 Triggered Reconciliation

A triggered reconciliation is required when any of the following occurs:

(a) A whistleblower or external complaint alleges a discrepancy between EDC
    records and CRO summaries;
(b) A site PI or treating physician reports a count discrepancy in writing;
(c) A regulatory authority requests a data integrity investigation;
(d) The sponsor's quality assurance team identifies a potential data quality issue.

For NT-7701 Phase II, the whistleblower complaint received on 2026-04-28 triggers
a mandatory reconciliation under this Section 4.2.

### 4.3 Reconciliation Documentation

All reconciliation activities must be documented in writing, including:

(a) The EDC-based Grade-3 SAE count per site and in aggregate;
(b) The CRO-reported Grade-3 SAE count per site and in aggregate;
(c) The discrepancy amount per site and in aggregate;
(d) The root cause of the discrepancy (if determinable);
(e) The corrective action taken or recommended.

This documentation must be retained in the Trial Master File for a minimum of
15 years from the date of study completion.
