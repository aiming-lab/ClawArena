## Executive Summary

NovaTherx Biosciences' NT-7701 Phase II randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled
clinical trial enrolled a total of 1,963 subjects across five investigator sites
(Bangalore, Berlin, Toronto, São Paulo, and Seoul). The study period covered
January 2025 through April 2026, with the final data freeze completed on 2026-04-15.

Based on the CRO's consolidated adverse event data, the overall safety profile
of NT-7701 appears consistent with the known pharmacological class of kinase
inhibitors. The incidence of Grade-3 and Grade-4 serious adverse events is
within the range expected for the patient population enrolled.

**CRO-reported Grade-3 SAE counts by site:**

| Site | Site Location | CRO Grade-3 SAE Count |
|---|---|---|
| site_01 | Bangalore | 12 |
| site_02 | Berlin | 10 |

| **TOTAL** | **All sites** | **47** |

These figures are derived from the CRO's consolidated summary table
(`cro_sae_summary.csv`) and reflect the CRO's internal adverse event coding
as of the data freeze reference date.

## 1. Study Design and Population

NT-7701 is a novel, selective kinase inhibitor under investigation as a treatment
for advanced solid tumours. The Phase II study was designed as a randomised,
double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with a 2:1 randomisation ratio (NT-7701
to placebo). The primary endpoint was objective response rate at 24 weeks, with
key secondary endpoints including progression-free survival, overall survival,
and safety (assessed by incidence and severity of adverse events per CTCAE v5.0).

The study enrolled adult patients aged 18 to 75 years with histologically confirmed
advanced solid tumours who had received at least one prior line of systemic therapy.
Key exclusion criteria included active autoimmune disease, prior treatment with
any kinase inhibitor in the same mechanistic class as NT-7701, and clinically
significant hepatic impairment (ALT or AST > 3× ULN at screening).

A total of 1,963 subjects were enrolled across the five sites: 420 at site_01
(Bangalore), 398 at site_02 (Berlin), 380 at site_03 (Toronto), 390 at site_04
(São Paulo), and 375 at site_05 (Seoul).

## 2. Overall Adverse Event Profile

During the study period, a total of 8,847 adverse events were recorded across
all sites and all grades. The distribution by grade was as follows:

| Grade | Event Count | % of All AEs |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 1 (Mild) | 5,219 | 59.0% |
| Grade 2 (Moderate) | 2,941 | 33.2% |
| Grade 3 (Severe/Significant) | 614 | 6.9% |
| Grade 4 (Life-threatening) | 73 | 0.8% |
| **Total** | **8,847** | **100%** |

The majority of adverse events were Grade 1 or Grade 2 and resolved without
medical intervention or with minimal supportive care. Grade 3 events that met
the SAE criteria required hospitalisation or prolonged existing hospitalisation
in all cases recorded across the five sites.

## 3. Grade-3 Serious Adverse Event Analysis



### 3.1 Site-Level Grade-3 SAE Overview

The following section provides a site-level narrative summary of Grade-3 SAEs
as recorded in the CRO's consolidated database. Note that these figures reflect
the CRO's coding as of 2026-04-16 and are subject to sponsor verification
against the primary EDC records.

**Site 01 — Bangalore (CRO count: 12 Grade-3 SAEs)**

Site 01 enrolled 420 subjects, of whom 420 completed the treatment period or
discontinued early as documented in the protocol-specified manner. The CRO
recorded 12 Grade-3 SAEs at this site. The most frequently reported Grade-3 terms
at site_01 included Pneumonitis (n=3), Hyponatremia (n=2), Elevated transaminases
(n=2), Peripheral neuropathy (n=2), and individual events of Thrombocytopenia,
Pulmonary embolism, and Febrile neutropenia (n=1 each). All events were managed
with standard of care and no Grade-3 SAE at this site resulted in a fatal outcome.

**Site 02 — Berlin (CRO count: 10 Grade-3 SAEs)**

Site 02 enrolled 398 subjects. The CRO recorded 10 Grade-3 SAEs. Reported terms
included Colitis (n=3), Elevated ALT (n=2), Skin rash requiring hospitalisation
(n=2), Hypertension crisis (n=2), and Renal impairment (n=1). The site's principal
investigator, Prof. Helga Braun, communicated with the sponsor's medical monitor
in April 2026 regarding the classification of several events; see
`pi_correspondence/email_thread_apr.md` for the relevant correspondence.

**Site 03 — Toronto (CRO count: 9 Grade-3 SAEs)**

Site 03 enrolled 380 subjects. The CRO recorded 9 Grade-3 SAEs. The CRO classified
three events with ae_term "Elevated liver enzymes" as Grade 2 based on a site query
received from the data management team. If these three events are reclassified as
Grade 3 per the treating physician's EDC entry, the site count would be 11. This
classification question is documented in `pi_correspondence/email_site_queries.md`.
The CRO's figure of 9 reflects their internal coding decision as of the data freeze.

**Site 04 — São Paulo (CRO count: 11 Grade-3 SAEs)**

Site 04 enrolled 390 subjects. The CRO recorded 11 Grade-3 SAEs. Events included
Hepatotoxicity (n=3), Pneumonitis (n=2), Deep vein thrombosis (n=2), Hyponatremia
(n=2), Atrial fibrillation (n=1), and Sepsis (n=1). No fatal outcomes were reported.

**Site 05 — Seoul (CRO count: 5 Grade-3 SAEs)**

Site 05 enrolled 375 subjects. The CRO recorded 5 Grade-3 SAEs. Events included
Immune-mediated colitis (n=2), Elevated creatinine (n=1), Peripheral neuropathy (n=1),
and Hypertension crisis (n=1). Site 05 had the lowest per-capita Grade-3 SAE rate
of the five sites, consistent with the site's patient population characteristics.

### 3.2 Protocol Amendment v2.1 and SAE Reporting Procedures

Protocol Amendment v2.1, which modified the SAE electronic reporting timelines,
was approved by the IRB on the date indicated on the IRB approval certificate.
The amendment requires site-level electronic confirmation of SAE onset within
48 hours, replacing the prior 72-hour window. All sites received written
notification of the amendment and updated their local reporting procedures
accordingly.

The IRB approval number associated with Protocol Amendment v2.1 is
IRB-2026-NT7701-003. The IRB chairperson for this study is Dr. Renata Okonkwo.

## 4. Grade-4 Serious Adverse Events

A total of 73 Grade-4 (life-threatening) serious adverse events were recorded
across all five sites. None of these events resulted in a fatal outcome within
the study observation period. Grade-4 events were managed with intensive
medical intervention and all subjects with Grade-4 events were discontinued
from study treatment per protocol requirements.
