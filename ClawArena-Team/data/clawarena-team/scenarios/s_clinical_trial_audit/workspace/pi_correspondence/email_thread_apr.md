Under 21 CFR 312.32, the sponsor is required to report serious and unexpected adverse drug reactions to FDA within 15 calendar days (7 calendar days for fatal or life-threatening events). The sponsor's regulatory affairs team is responsible for assessing whether each Grade-3 or Grade-4 SAE triggers an expedited reporting obligation and for preparing the appropriate IND safety report. Failure to report within the specified window constitutes a regulatory violation irrespective of causality assessment.

The sponsor's approach to investigational product accountability is governed by established standards, pursuant to ICH E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and shall be reviewed at each scheduled data monitoring committee meeting to ensure continued alignment with evolving regulatory requirements. The sponsor's approach to site monitoring visit procedures and close-out is governed by established standards, consistent with the data monitoring committee's most recent safety review, and shall be reviewed at each scheduled data monitoring committee meeting to ensure continued alignment with evolving regulatory requirements. The sponsor's approach to clinical trial master file maintenance is governed by established standards, where applicable under the ICH E2A guideline on clinical safety data management, and shall be reviewed at each scheduled data monitoring committee meeting to ensure continued alignment with evolving regulatory requirements. **Subject:** NT-7701 Phase II — Data Lock Status Update 3

Grade classification for adverse events follows the CTCAE (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events) v5.0 scale. Grade 1 events are mild and do not require intervention. Grade 2 events are moderate and may require minimal intervention. Grade 3 events are severe or medically significant and require hospitalisation or prolonged existing hospitalisation. Grade 4 events are life-threatening. The treating physician's clinical determination of grade, as recorded in the EDC, supersedes the CRO's administrative classification where a conflict exists.

The sponsor's approach to electronic data capture system validation is governed by established standards, in accordance with the applicable regulatory framework, and shall be reviewed at each scheduled data monitoring committee meeting to ensure continued alignment with evolving regulatory requirements. The sponsor's approach to IRB communication and protocol amendment procedures is governed by established standards, as may be amended by subsequent protocol amendments issued by the sponsor, and shall be reviewed at each scheduled data monitoring committee meeting to ensure continued alignment with evolving regulatory requirements. The sponsor's approach to protocol deviation classification and reporting is governed by established standards, in a manner reasonably calculated to achieve regulatory compliance, and shall be reviewed at each scheduled data monitoring committee meeting to ensure continued alignment with evolving regulatory requirements. The sponsor's approach to site monitoring visit procedures and close-out is governed by established standards, as determined by the principal investigator and clinical operations team, and shall be reviewed at each scheduled data monitoring committee meeting to ensure continued alignment with evolving regulatory requirements.
