# FDA 5-Day Expedited SAE Reporting Criteria
# Reference: 21 CFR 312.32 and FDA Guidance for Industry
# Reproduced for internal reference — NovaTherx Regulatory Affairs

---

## 1. Overview

Under 21 CFR 312.32, sponsors of investigational new drug (IND) applications are
required to report certain serious and unexpected adverse drug reactions to FDA
on an expedited basis. The regulatory framework distinguishes between 7-calendar-day
reports (for fatal or immediately life-threatening events) and 15-calendar-day
reports (for serious, unexpected events that are not fatal or immediately
life-threatening). In certain limited circumstances, 5-calendar-day reports are
required.

---

## 2. 5-Day Reporting Obligation

A 5-calendar-day report is required when the sponsor concludes that there is
reasonable evidence that the IND study drug is causing a serious and unexpected
adverse drug reaction AND one or more of the following circumstances applies:

(a) FDA specifically requests a 5-day report for a particular type of event;
(b) The event meets criteria specified in the FDA's clinical hold regulations
    (21 CFR 312.42) as a potential basis for a clinical hold; or
(c) A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) or Data Monitoring Committee (DMC)
    recommends that the sponsor consider immediate reporting based on unblinded
    safety data.

---

## 3. Applicability to Grade-3 SAEs

Not all Grade-3 SAEs trigger a 5-day reporting obligation. The key criteria are:

1. **Seriousness:** The event must be serious as defined under ICH E2A.
2. **Unexpectedness:** The event must not be listed in the Investigator's Brochure
   (IB) or protocol safety sections as an expected event at the severity observed.
3. **Causality:** There must be a reasonable possibility of a causal relationship
   between the event and the study drug (a.k.a. "suspected" ADR).

For NT-7701 Phase II, the following Grade-3 AE terms are listed in the current
Investigator's Brochure as expected events: Pneumonitis, Elevated transaminases,
Colitis, Skin rash, Thrombocytopenia. These listed events do not trigger a
15-day report (and certainly not a 5-day report) unless there is a significant
increase in frequency or severity beyond what is described in the IB.

---

## 4. Implications for NT-7701 Audit

The confirmed Grade-3 SAE under-count identified in the NovaTherx audit (if
verified by the EDC reconciliation) does not necessarily trigger expedited
reporting obligations for each individually under-counted event, provided:

(a) Each under-counted Grade-3 event, on its own, does not meet the unexpectedness
    criterion (i.e., the ae_term is listed in the IB);
(b) The systematic nature of the under-count does not itself constitute a safety
    signal requiring expedited notification.

However, the sponsor's regulatory affairs team (specifically DR_SCHULTZ and
MARCUS_YUEN) must assess each under-counted event against the three criteria
above before concluding that no expedited reports are triggered. This assessment
must be documented in the audit findings.
