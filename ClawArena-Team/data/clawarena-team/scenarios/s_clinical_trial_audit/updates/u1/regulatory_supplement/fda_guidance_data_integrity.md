## 1. Purpose

FDA's guidance on data integrity addresses how current good manufacturing practice
(CGMP) regulations apply to data integrity requirements. For clinical trial data,
analogous data integrity principles apply under 21 CFR Part 312 and ICH E6(R3).

---

## 2. Data Integrity Principles — ALCOA+

FDA expects that clinical trial data be Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous,
Original, and Accurate (ALCOA), plus Complete, Consistent, Enduring, and Available
(ALCOA+).

### 2.1 Attributable

Data must be attributable to the person who entered it, and changes to data must
be attributable to the person who made the change. The EDC audit trail provides
the primary mechanism for ensuring attributability.

### 2.2 Legible

Data must be legible and permanent. Electronic data must be capable of being
read without specialised tools not generally available to inspectors.

### 2.3 Contemporaneous

Data must be recorded at the time the activity or observation was made. Adverse
event data must be entered into the EDC promptly after the event is observed and
documented in the source record.

### 2.4 Original

The original record (or a certified true copy) must be preserved. For electronic
data, the audit trail constitutes the means by which the original data can be
distinguished from subsequent modifications.

### 2.5 Accurate

Data must be accurate. CRO-generated summary tables derived from EDC data must
accurately reflect the underlying EDC records. Systematic discrepancies between
CRO summaries and EDC records are data integrity failures.

---

## 3. EDC vs. CRO Summary — Authority Hierarchy

In the context of FDA-regulated clinical trials, the electronic data capture
system records serve as the authoritative data source. Summary tables generated
by a CRO from EDC data are derived documents and do not supersede the EDC records.
Where a discrepancy exists, the EDC records must be treated as the authoritative
source, and the discrepancy must be investigated and resolved.
